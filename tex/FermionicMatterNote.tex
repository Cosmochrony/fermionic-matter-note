  \begin{abstract}
    The fermionic matter sub-programme asks where fermions, chirality, hypercharge and three generations
    could enter the Cosmochrony framework.
    Its answer is layered and each layer names its assumptions.
    A supplied dictionary $F_n\simeq\mathcal{H}_c$ concerns the Heisenberg carrier;
    the associated Weil block $V_c\simeq\mathbb{C}^q$ and supplied spinor doublet
    $V_\rho\simeq\mathbb{C}^2$ are distinct, with no selection map constructed.
    Given a real metaplectic model and the doublet, the algebra is proved without a metric:
    $\mathfrak{mp}(2,\mathbb{R})_\mathbb{C}\simeq\mathfrak{sl}_2(\mathbb{C})$,
    the symmetric square is the adjoint and the exterior square the determinant line.
    [H-Spin] identifies the doublet action with the spin group of a four-dimensional Lorentzian co-metric;
    [H-Weak] supplies a distinct rank-two weak factor with structure group $U(2)$.
    Neither identification is supplied by a source or excluded by a theorem.
    Connection-dependent admissible readings retain Q6a's two unproved continuum hypotheses:
    a smooth effective fibre bundle and a differentiable spatial continuum, the latter open in Q5a.
    Lorentz chirality and weak chiral selection are different statements.
    Left-admissibility is an input orientation branch given the Born--Infeld datum;
    weak $V{-}A$ is the representation content of [H-Weak], not derived from that branch.
    Hypercharge constraints need both spin and weak hypotheses; rigidity also uses a supplied colour module
    and matter decomposition, and the Standard Model pattern needs minimal determinant-line normalisation.
    A supplied rank-three rule gives a conditional gauge-singlet generation factor.
    Generation operators are models on a distinct doublet; their reading as restrictions of $E_\Pi^2$
    requires [H-Res], the level-to-generation map is open, and the split value is matching data.
    The Schur remainder equals the projective endomorphism only under an unsupplied symbol identity;
    a non-zero split, its Schur transport and Born--Infeld genus remain hypotheses.
    O31 supplies no colour derivation.
    NSR excludes its two deposited candidates for an independent weak multiplicity by exact obstructions;
    this does not exclude every extension, and the weak factor remains an architectural input.
    \emph{Interpretive outlook (a reading, not a result).}
    The programme locates the missing connection between internal algebra, Lorentz frames and weak
    multiplicity, rather than deriving the observed fermionic content.
  \end{abstract}

  \section{Motivation and Central Question}
    \label{sec:motivation}

    \paragraph{Convention for \texorpdfstring{$J_\Pi$}{J-Pi}.}
    The notation $J_\Pi$ is interpreted on the carrier specified in each statement.
    On the generation carrier $\Cgen$, it denotes the internal antilinear parity of Q14 Section~6.
    In the spinorial statements, including the EBJ subsection and the relation
    $R_b=\varphi\circ J_\Pi^{-1}$, it denotes the chirality-reversing spinorial parity:
    $J_\Pi\gamma_5=-\gamma_5J_\Pi$, under the hypotheses of the spinorial lift.
    This is the convention used in Q11-OF and CHO~\cite{Beau2026q11of, Beau2026cho}.
    The internal parity on $\Cgen$ alone does not supply a spinorial lift or this chirality relation.

    The fermionic matter sub-programme answers the following question.

    \medskip
    \noindent\textit{Central question.}
    The bosonic spectral stratification --- gravity at $a_2$, Yang--Mills at $a_4$ --- derives
    the bosonic sector of the Standard Model from the same admissible spectral functional.
    Fermions, chirality, hypercharge assignments, and the three-generation structure are not
    covered by the bosonic synthesis.
    \textit{Do these fermionic structures also arise as forced consequences of the admissible
    Weil fibre, or must they be introduced as additional postulates?}

    \medskip
    The answer given in Q14~\cite{Beau2026q14} is stratified, not uniform.
    It is stated throughout relative to a \emph{supplied} Heisenberg carrier and a fixed non-trivial
    central character.
    Foundation distinguishes the Heisenberg carrier $\mathcal{H}_c$, the associated Weil block
    $V_c \simeq \mathbb{C}^q$, and the supplied spinor doublet $\Vr \simeq \mathbb{C}^2$.
    No map selecting the doublet from that block is constructed.
    The identification $F_n \overset{\mathrm{model}}{\simeq} \mathcal{H}_c$ is not selected by the admissibility
    axioms, which give irreducibility and non-commutation only under a representation-theoretic dictionary and
    an additional sector reading of A3, not derived, and neither make the commutator central
    nor single out a finite Heisenberg group, an explicit $\mathfrak{S}_3$ countermodel satisfying
    the extracted carrier contract~\cite{Beau2026m, Beau2026HeisenbergStructure}.
    On that carrier the finite Weil module acts through $\SL(2,\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z})$, so a real metaplectic
    model and a doublet carrying its Lie algebra are supplied model data.
    Given them, the algebra is proved and needs no metric: $\mathrm{mp}(2,\mathbb{R})_\mathbb{C} \simeq
    \mathfrak{sl}_2(\mathbb{C})$, the symmetric square of the doublet is the adjoint and its exterior square the
    determinant line.
    Two physical readings of this algebra are separate hypotheses, each a missing identification and neither
    excluded by any result.
    \HSpin{} (Hypothesis~2.2 of Q14) identifies the $\SL(2,\mathbb{C})$ acting on the doublet with the spin group of
    a four-dimensional Lorentzian co-metric on the effective base, and no source supplies it: the geometric branch
    supplies only a rank-three degenerate co-metric, under its hypotheses, and nothing relates the metaplectic
    action to its
    frames.
    \HWeak{} (Hypothesis~2.3 of Q14) supplies a distinct rank-two weak factor $\Ew$ with structure group $\UU(2)$,
    which Q14 does not construct: by Schur's lemma a weak action commuting with Lorentz transformations cannot live
    on the doublet that carries the spin reading.
    Under \HSpin{}, spinoriality is a statement about spacetime spinors, $\mathrm{Sym}^2(S_L)$ is the Lorentz sector,
    and $\wedge^2(S_L)$ is a trivial line carrying no hypercharge.
    Under \HWeak{}, the same tensor functors applied to $\Ew$ give the weak adjoint, whose compact real form is
    $\su(2)_L$, and the determinant line $L_Y := \wedge^2(\Ew)$, which carries $\UU(1)_Y$ non-trivially only because
    the structure group of $\Ew$ is $\UU(2)$.
    Chirality splits in two.
    Lorentz chirality is the statement that the projective endomorphism $\Epi$ of the projected Dirac operator is
    left-admissible, $P_R \Epi P_R = 0$: under \HSpin{}, the Born--Infeld datum (H1)--(H2) of O30 and the
    orientation-compatible branch, which is an input.
    The weak chiral selection, the $V-A$ structure, is the representation content of \HWeak{}, and Q14 does not
    derive it from left-admissibility.
    Hypercharge does not stand on the same footing, and it separates into two steps.
    The anomaly-cancellation constraints, which require both hypotheses, are weighted by the representation
    dimension, so the rigidity of the hypercharge weights carries a supplied three-dimensional colour module as a
    hypothesis; Q14 neither analyses the constraint system at general multiplicity nor claims that
    the constraints force three.
    Selecting the Standard Model pattern from those constraints needs a further premise: that the
    determinant line $L_Y = \wedge^2(\Ew)$ carries the minimal integral normalisation
    compatible with the $\UU(1)_Y$ action on the projected spinorial sectors.
    Under that premise, and on the Standard Model content without a right-handed neutrino and with $Y_e \neq 0$, the
    pattern is selected only up to an overall sign, rescaling and the exchange of the labels $u_R$ and $d_R$, the
    cubic constraint vanishing on it precisely at colour multiplicity three.
    The constraints are the gauge-curvature ones only: the $\Epi$-dependent traces are not analysed in Q14.
    Q14 records the explicit connection of that normalisation to the pattern as an open direction:
    the anomaly constraints restrict the allowed weights, while the determinant-line normalisation
    fixes their scale.
    Three generations arise conditionally from the spinorial multiplicity reading of the invariant
    $\sigma_c(n_3) = 3$, a supplied selection rule (O23 proves the three-dimensionality of the neutral sector of a
    supplied spinor carrier, Theorem~3.1; the carrier selection and the identification of $\Sigma_c$ with
    that dimension are open).
    The resulting factor $\Cgen$ is distinct from the Lorentz sector and from any spatial triplet: no source
    identifies the invariant with spatial directions.
    What is NOT supplied is the independent fermionic weak doublet: the tensor functors of the
    spinor bundle give Lorentz-typed adjoint and determinant data, and under \HSpin{} the Lorentz commutant of the
    irreducible Weyl factor is scalar, so no internal non-abelian factor acts on the same spinor factor.
    The two deposited candidates for the missing multiplicity carrier $M_L \simeq \mathbb{C}^2$
    --- the internal Hecke operator doublets and the erased history fibres --- are excluded by
    exact obstructions in the companion no-go note (NSR)~\cite{Beau2026nsr}, whose central result is
    the no-sequential-re-entry theorem: information erased by the projective channel cannot be
    recovered by any tower of subsequent projections.
    NSR's finite-stratum obstructions are unconditional (not conditional on \HSpin{}); its Lorentz-lock input is
    imported from Q14, where the spinor reading of the carrier is under \HSpin{}.
    The matter-sector weak doublet, the role that this note reads the factor $\Ew$ of \HWeak{} as playing (a reading
    of this note, stated by neither Q14 nor the no-go note), is therefore an explicitly open frontier,
    realisable through an architectural extension, such as a parallel substrate channel with an oriented
    central/Weyl datum or an independent new multiplicity carrier, which the no-go note does not exclude, and
    not a consequence of the deposited corpus.

    \medskip
    This sub-programme is the first paper of the Q-series that depends simultaneously on all
    four layers of the programme: the admissible Weil fibre structure on the supplied carrier
    (Notes~1 and~5), the conditional Lorentzian geometry (Note~2), the gauge bundle for a supplied
    compact structure group (Note~3), and the conditional gauge--gravity
    system (Q13~\cite{Beau2026q13}, summarised in Presentation Note~8).

  \section{Logical Chain of the Sub-Programme}
    \label{sec:chain}

    The sub-programme is organised around the following structural chain, whose first arrow is read
    on the supplied Heisenberg carrier of Section~\ref{sec:motivation} rather than derived from the
    admissibility axioms, and whose second arrow is a modelling step.

    \begin{equation}
      \label{eq:chain}
      \Pi \leadsto F_n \overset{\mathrm{model}}{\simeq} \mathcal{H}_c
      \;\overset{\text{model}}{\leadsto}\;
      \Mp(2,\mathbb{R})
      \;\Rightarrow\;
      \mathrm{mp}(2,\mathbb{R})_\mathbb{C} \simeq \mathfrak{sl}_2(\mathbb{C})
      \;\overset{\HSpin}{\leadsto}\;
      \Spin(3,1) \simeq \SL(2,\mathbb{C})
      \;\Rightarrow\;
      \Sbundle
    \end{equation}
    The arrow marked \HSpin{} is not an implication: it is Hypothesis~2.2 of Q14, supplied by no source.
    Given \HSpin{} and the distinct weak factor of \HWeak{} (Hypothesis~2.3 of Q14), the readings of the tensor
    sectors, of chirality and of generation multiplicity are:
    \[
      \underbrace{\mathrm{Sym}^2(S_L) \simeq \mathrm{ad}(P_\Pi),\ \wedge^2(S_L)\ \text{trivial}}
        _{\HSpin\text{: Lorentz sector}}
      \;,\quad
      \underbrace{\mathrm{Sym}^2(\Ew) \Rightarrow \adC(\su(2)_L)}_{\HWeak\text{: }\SU(2)_L}
      \;,\quad
      \underbrace{\wedge^2(\Ew) = L_Y}_{\HWeak\text{: }\UU(1)_Y}
      \;,
    \]
    \[
      \underbrace{\Epi \text{ left-admissible}}_{\text{Lorentz chirality, }\HSpin}
      \;,\quad
      \underbrace{\text{left-handed doublet assignment}}_{V-A,\ \HWeak}
      \;,\quad
      \underbrace{\sigma_c(n_3)=3 \to \Cgen}_{\text{3 generations (supplied rule)}}.
    \]

    Three distinct mechanisms contribute.

    \begin{enumerate}
      \item \textit{The complexified metaplectic algebra is $\mathfrak{sl}_2(\mathbb{C})$; its spin reading is
      \HSpin{}.}
      The associated symplectic action on the finite Weil block $V_c$ factors through the finite group $\SL(2,\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z})$, which has no Lie algebra.
      A real metaplectic model $\Mp(2,\mathbb{R})$ and the action of its Lie algebra on a two-dimensional doublet
      are therefore supplied model data.
      Given them, the complexification $\mathrm{mp}(2,\mathbb{R})_\mathbb{C} \simeq \mathfrak{sl}_2(\mathbb{C})$ is a
      statement about Lie algebras: it is algebraic and independent of any metric, and its simply connected
      integration is $\SL(2,\mathbb{C}) \simeq \Spin(3,1)$.
      An abstract isomorphism does not make this group the spin group of the effective spacetime.
      That requires a Lorentzian co-metric on the effective base and an identification of the $\SL(2,\mathbb{C})$
      acting on the doublet with the group covering the Lorentz frames of that co-metric, which is \HSpin{}.
      The spinor bundle $\Sbundle = S_L \oplus S_R$ is the associated bundle under \HSpin{}.

      \item \textit{The tensor functors are algebra on the supplied doublet; their readings are two distinct
      hypotheses.}
      $\mathrm{Sym}^2(\mathbb{C}^2) \simeq \mathfrak{sl}_2(\mathbb{C})$ is the complex adjoint and
      $\wedge^2(\mathbb{C}^2) \simeq \det(\mathbb{C}^2)$ the determinant line, trivial on $\SL(2,\mathbb{C})$.
      On the spinor factor under \HSpin{}, $\mathrm{Sym}^2(S_L)$ is the Lorentz sector and $\wedge^2(S_L)$ a trivial
      line that carries no hypercharge: neither is electroweak data.
      The electroweak reading applies the same functors to a distinct rank-two factor $\Ew$ with structure group
      $\UU(2)$ (\HWeak{}): $\mathrm{Sym}^2(\Ew)$ contains the complex weak adjoint, as an $\SU(2)$-module (as a
      $\UU(2)$-bundle it is that adjoint twisted by $L_Y$), from which the compact real form selects $\su(2)_L$, and
      $L_Y := \wedge^2(\Ew)$ supports $\UU(1)_Y$, non-trivially only because the structure
      group is $\UU(2)$.
      By the calibration of~\cite{Beau2026nsr}, no such factor is produced by the functors on $\Sbundle$: a fermionic
      weak doublet requires a multiplicity factor $M_L \simeq \mathbb{C}^2$ commuting with the Lorentz action,
      the Lorentz commutant of the irreducible Weyl factor being scalar under \HSpin{}.
      In the reading of this note $\Ew$ is the role such a factor would play: it is posited by \HWeak{} and not
      constructed.

      \item \textit{The projective endomorphism $\Epi$ gives Lorentz chirality; the weak chiral selection and the
      hypercharge constraints need \HWeak{} as well.}
      The projected Dirac operator, under \HSpin{}, satisfies the Lichnerowicz-type identity
      \[
        D^2_{\Pi,g,A}
        = -(\nabla^{S,A})^2 + \frac{R}{4} + \frac{1}{2}\gamma^\mu\gamma^\nu F^a_{\mu\nu} T^a_R
        + \Epi.
      \]
      Under \HSpin{} and the Born--Infeld datum (H1)--(H2) of O30, Q14~\cite{Beau2026q14} derives a unique
      antilinear lift of the parity that reverses chirality.
      Left-admissibility ($P_R \Epi P_R = 0$) is the definition of the orientation-compatible branch of Q14
      (Proposition~3.8), an input that the lift does not select.
      On that branch a non-zero residue must break the $J_\Pi$ parity for $\Epi\preceq0$, an explicit hypothesis of Q14
      (Q14, Proposition~3.8): for the Schur residue of a
      $J_\Pi$-commuting locking operator, under the chiral splitting and \HGen{}(i) of~\cite{Beau2026prs},
      left-admissibility forces the residue to vanish.
      This is a statement about the projective residue and the Lorentz chirality $\gamma_5$.
      The $V-A$ structure is a different statement: the weak gauge action is carried by the left-handed factor
      alone, which is the representation content of \HWeak{}, and Q14 does not derive it from left-admissibility
      (Remark~3.9 of Q14).
      Under \HSpin{}, \HWeak{} and symbol compatibility, $\UU(1)_Y$ invariance of $\SPI[g,A,D_{\Pi,g,A}]$ imposes the
      anomaly-cancellation trace constraint $\mathrm{Tr}_{\Sbundle}(\gamma_5 Y A_\Pi(x)) = 0$ on the hypercharge
      weights, in its gauge-curvature part; the $\Epi$-dependent traces are not analysed in Q14.
      That constraint carries no colour hypothesis.
      The supplied three-dimensional colour module enters at the next level, that of the rigidity of
      the weights: the anomaly sums are weighted by the representation dimension, so the colour
      multiplicity enters them.
      Selecting the Standard Model pattern from those constraints is a further step, under the
      additional premise that $L_Y = \wedge^2(\Ew)$ carries the minimal integral normalisation compatible with the
      $\UU(1)_Y$ action, on the Standard Model content without a right-handed neutrino and with $Y_e \neq 0$, and then
      only up to an overall sign, rescaling and the exchange of the labels $u_R$ and $d_R$; the cubic constraint
      vanishes on that pattern precisely at colour multiplicity three.
    \end{enumerate}

    \subsection{Q14: Toward fermionic matter from projective Dirac admissibility}
      \label{sec:q14}

      \textbf{Q14}~\cite{Beau2026q14} is the sole constituent paper.
      It is organised around three independent but mutually compatible results, whose hypotheses differ.

      \textit{Theorem~A: Spinorial electroweak bundle} (algebra proved on supplied model data; two conditional
      readings).
      (a)~On the supplied Heisenberg carrier, given a real metaplectic model and a supplied doublet $V$, the
      complexified metaplectic algebra is $\mathfrak{sl}_2(\mathbb{C})$, with
      \[
        \mathrm{Sym}^2(V) \simeq \mathfrak{sl}_2(\mathbb{C}), \qquad \wedge^2(V) \simeq \mathbb{C}_{\det},
      \]
      the adjoint and the trivial line; this is algebraic, proved without either hypothesis, and independent of any
      metric.
      (b)~Under \HSpin{}, $\Sbundle = S_L \oplus S_R$ is a Dirac spinor bundle of the effective Lorentzian co-metric,

  \section{Conclusion}
    \label{sec:conclusion}

    The fermionic matter sub-programme states what the fermionic sector requires when fermions are sought in the
    admissible Weil fibre rather than added to the Cosmochrony framework as external fields.
    On a supplied Heisenberg carrier, given a real metaplectic model and a supplied doublet, the algebra is proved
    and uses no metric: the complexified metaplectic algebra is $\mathfrak{sl}_2(\mathbb{C})$, the symmetric square of
    the doublet is the adjoint and its exterior square the determinant line.
    The dictionary $F_n \overset{\mathrm{model}}{\simeq} \mathcal{H}_c$, the Weil action on $V_c$ and
    the supplied doublet $\Vr$ are distinct inputs; no map selecting that doublet is constructed.
    Connection-dependent admissible interpretations retain both unproved continuum hypotheses of Q6a:
    a smooth effective fibre bundle and differentiable spatial continuum, the latter open in Q5a.
    The carrier is supplied rather than selected by the admissibility axioms, and everything below
    inherits that condition.
    Two physical readings of the algebra are distinct hypotheses, each a missing identification and neither excluded
    by any result: \HSpin{}, which identifies the $\SL(2,\mathbb{C})$ of the doublet with the spin group of a
    four-dimensional Lorentzian co-metric on the effective base, and \HWeak{}, which supplies a distinct rank-two weak
    factor $\Ew$ with structure group $\UU(2)$.

    Under \HSpin{}, $\Sbundle = S_L \oplus S_R$ is a spinor bundle of the effective co-metric, $\mathrm{Sym}^2(S_L)$ is
    the Lorentz sector and $\wedge^2(S_L)$ a trivial line (Theorem~A).
    Under \HWeak{}, the electroweak data of $\SU(2)_L \times \UU(1)_Y$ arise from the same tensor functors applied to
    $\Ew$: $\su(2)_L \subset \mathrm{Sym}^2(\Ew)$ and $L_Y = \wedge^2(\Ew)$.
    The independent fermionic weak doublet, the role that this note reads $\Ew$ as playing, is not produced by the
      functors on
    $\Sbundle$ and not constructed, its two deposited candidates being excluded at the operator and history strata
    by the companion no-go note~\cite{Beau2026nsr}.
    Lorentz chirality, the left-admissibility of the projective endomorphism $\Epi$, is the definition of the
    orientation-compatible branch of Q14, an input, under \HSpin{} and the Born--Infeld datum of O30 (Theorem~B); on
    that branch a non-zero residue breaks the $J_\Pi$ parity of the lift for $\Epi\preceq0$, an explicit hypothesis of Q14.
    The $V-A$ structure is a different statement, the representation content of \HWeak{}, and it is not derived from
    left-admissibility.
    Under both hypotheses, the anomaly-cancellation trace constrains the hypercharge weights with no colour

    \paragraph{Interpretive outlook.}
    The following is a reading of the results, not a result.
    The Standard Model does not treat spin and weak isospin as one structure: a left-handed electron is a Lorentz
    spinor and, separately, a member of a weak doublet.
    The sub-programme reaches the same separation from the other side.
    A supplied doublet on the admissible fibre carries one copy of the algebra, and that algebra is exactly the algebra
    of both spin and weak isospin, but one copy cannot carry both roles.
    Where the fermionic sector meets spacetime and where it meets the weak interaction are therefore two distinct
    questions, and the question of why weak interactions are left-handed becomes the question of how the two factors
    are joined.
    The remaining problems are specific: the two hypotheses on how the fibre is soldered, and the rigidity problems
    for $\Epi$ and $L_Y$.
