  \begin{abstract}
    The fermionic matter sub-programme of the Cosmochrony corpus addresses a single
    central question: where do fermions, chirality, hypercharge, and three generations come
    from?
    The answer is stratified and conditional.
    They need not be postulated as external fields: they arise as the spinorial face of the Weil
    module $\Vr$ carried by the admissible fibre on a \emph{supplied} Heisenberg carrier, once its
    metaplectic structure is complexified by the Lorentzian metric of the geometric branch.
    The admissibility axioms do not select that carrier, and every result below inherits it.
    The structural chain, read on it, is:
    \[
      \Pi \Rightarrow F_n \simeq \Vr \Rightarrow \Mp(2,\mathbb{R})
      \Rightarrow \mathrm{mp}(2,\mathbb{R})_\mathbb{C} \simeq \mathfrak{sl}_2(\mathbb{C})
      \rightsquigarrow \Spin(3,1) \simeq \SL(2,\mathbb{C})
      \Rightarrow \Sbundle.
    \]
    Three modular structural results are established in Q14: (A)~the tensor functors of $\Sbundle$
    produce the gauge-sector data of $\SU(2)_L \times \UU(1)_Y$, Lorentz-typed adjoint and
    determinant data rather than an independent fermionic weak doublet carrier;
    (B)~the projective endomorphism $\Epi$ of the projected Dirac operator enforces the $V-A$ chiral
    structure, and its $\gamma_5$-weighted $a_4$ coefficient imposes anomaly-cancellation constraints
    on the hypercharge weights; (C)~the saturation invariant $\sigma_c(n_3) = 3$ yields a
    gauge-singlet three-generation factor $\Cgen$, conditionally on the supplied rank-three carrier.
    Their hypotheses differ, and (B) is itself stratified.
    The $V-A$ structure carries nothing beyond the supplied carrier.
    The rigidity of the hypercharge weights carries a supplied three-dimensional colour module, the
    anomaly sums being weighted by the representation dimension.
    Selecting the Standard Model pattern carries, beyond that module, the minimal integral
    normalisation of $L_Y = \wedge^2(\Sbundle)$, holds only up to an overall sign and rescaling, and
    rests on a connection Q14 leaves open; the quark sector carries the module but not that
    normalisation.
    O31 is a withdrawal notice: no colour triplet is established as admissible, $\SU(3)$ is not
    identified, and no gauge factor is derived.
    The structural form of $\Epi$ is fixed as a Schur complement of the eliminated spinorial block
    and the generation-split mechanism is Born--Infeld saturation, but the split value is
    dictionary-bound, its first-principles front lying upstream in the ADE case selection and the
    cascade exponent.
    A dedicated no-go note closes the two deposited candidates for the independent fermionic weak
    multiplicity $M_L \simeq \mathbb{C}^2$ through the no-sequential-re-entry theorem, leaving the
    matter-sector weak doublet an explicit architectural extension rather than a derivation.
  \end{abstract}

  \section{Motivation and Central Question}
    \label{sec:motivation}

    The fermionic matter sub-programme answers the following question.

    \medskip
    \noindent\textit{Central question.}
    The bosonic spectral stratification — gravity at $a_2$, Yang--Mills at $a_4$ --- derives
    the bosonic sector of the Standard Model from the same admissible spectral functional.
    Fermions, chirality, hypercharge assignments, and the three-generation structure are not
    covered by the bosonic synthesis.
    \textit{Do these fermionic structures also arise as forced consequences of the admissible
    Weil fibre, or must they be introduced as additional postulates?}

    \medskip
    The answer established in Q14~\cite{Beau2026q14} is stratified, not uniform.
    It is stated throughout relative to a \emph{supplied} Heisenberg carrier and a fixed non-trivial
    central character: the identification $F_n \simeq \Vr$ is not selected by the admissibility
    axioms, which force irreducibility and non-commutation but neither make the commutator central
    nor single out a finite Heisenberg group, an explicit $\mathfrak{S}_3$ countermodel satisfying
    the extracted carrier contract~\cite{Beau2026m, Beau2026HeisenbergStructure}.
    On that carrier, fermions arise as the spinorial face of the Weil module $\Vr$ after Lorentzian
    complexification of its metaplectic lift: spinoriality follows.
    Chirality and the $V-A$ structure arise from the projective endomorphism $\Epi$ of the
    projected Dirac operator, via the spinorial lift of BI parity established in Q14: the
    chiral selection follows, and carries no further hypothesis.
    Hypercharge does not stand on the same footing, and it separates into two steps.
    The anomaly-cancellation constraints are weighted by the representation dimension, so the
    rigidity of the hypercharge weights carries a supplied three-dimensional colour module as a
    hypothesis; Q14 neither analyses the constraint system at general multiplicity nor claims that
    the constraints force three.
    Selecting the Standard Model pattern from those constraints needs a further premise: that the
    determinant line $L_Y = \wedge^2(\Sbundle)$ carries the minimal integral normalisation
    compatible with the $\UU(1)_Y$ action on the projected spinorial sectors.
    Under that premise the pattern is selected only up to an overall sign and rescaling, the cubic
    constraint vanishing on it precisely at colour multiplicity three.
    Q14 records the explicit connection of that normalisation to the pattern as an open direction:
    the anomaly constraints restrict the allowed weights, while the determinant-line normalisation
    fixes their scale.
    Three generations arise, conditionally on the supplied rank-three carrier, from the
    spinorial multiplicity reading of the same invariant $\sigma_c(n_3) = 3$ that yields the
    three spatial directions in the geometric branch; the threshold is a supplied selection
    rule (O23 proves the three-dimensionality of the neutral sector of a supplied spinor
    carrier, Theorem~3.1; the carrier selection and the identification of $\Sigma_c$ with
    that dimension are open).
    What is NOT forced at the current stratum is the independent fermionic weak doublet: the
    tensor functors of $\Sbundle$ produce gauge-sector (adjoint and determinant) data typed by
    the Lorentz action, and the Lorentz commutant of the irreducible Weyl factor is scalar, so
    no internal non-abelian factor acts on the same spinor factor.
    The two deposited candidates for the missing multiplicity carrier $M_L \simeq \mathbb{C}^2$
    --- the internal Hecke operator doublets and the erased history fibres --- are closed by
    exact obstructions in the companion no-go note~\cite{Beau2026nsr}, whose central result is
    the no-sequential-re-entry theorem: information erased by the projective channel cannot be
    recovered by any tower of subsequent projections.
    The matter-sector weak doublet is therefore an explicitly open frontier, realisable only
    through a parallel substrate channel with an oriented central/Weyl datum --- a stated
    architectural extension, not a consequence of the deposited corpus.

    \medskip
    This sub-programme is the first paper of the Q-series that depends simultaneously on all
    four layers of the programme: the admissible Weil fibre structure on the supplied carrier
    (Notes~1 and~5), the closed Lorentzian geometry (Note~2), the gauge bundle for a supplied
    compact structure group (Note~3), and the conditional gauge--gravity
    system (Q13~\cite{Beau2026q13}, summarised in Presentation Note~8).

    \begin{remark}[Scope: identification only, not mass spectrum]

spinor bundle, chirality, hypercharge quantum numbers, and generation factor.
The spinor bundle and the chiral selection are settled on the supplied Heisenberg carrier.
The generation factor remains conditional on a supplied rank-three carrier.
The hypercharge quantum numbers split in two: the rigidity of the weights is conditional on a

It does not derive the mass spectrum, the Yukawa couplings, or the CKM/PMNS mixing
matrices.
Those require the explicit construction of $\Epi$ as an operator on the spectral space,
and the integration of the projected Yukawa Lagrangian $\mathcal{L}_Y$, both of which
are listed as open deliverables.
    \end{remark}

  \section{Logical Chain of the Sub-Programme}
    \label{sec:chain}

    The sub-programme is organised around the following structural chain, whose first arrow is read
    on the supplied Heisenberg carrier of Section~\ref{sec:motivation} rather than derived from the
    admissibility axioms.

    \begin{equation}
      \label{eq:chain}
      \Pi \Rightarrow F_n \simeq \Vr
      \;\Rightarrow\;
      \Mp(2,\mathbb{R})
      \;\Rightarrow\;
      \mathrm{mp}(2,\mathbb{R})_\mathbb{C} \simeq \mathfrak{sl}_2(\mathbb{C})
      \;\rightsquigarrow\;
      \Spin(3,1) \simeq \SL(2,\mathbb{C})
      \;\Rightarrow\;
      \Sbundle
    \end{equation}
    \[
      \;\Rightarrow\;
      \underbrace{\mathrm{Sym}^2(\Sbundle) \Rightarrow \adC(P_\Pi)}_{\SU(2)_L}
      \;,\quad
      \underbrace{\wedge^2(\Sbundle) = L_Y}_{\UU(1)_Y}
      \;,\quad
      \underbrace{\Epi \text{ left-admissible}}_{V-A}
      \;,\quad
      \underbrace{\sigma_c(n_3)=3 \to \Cgen}_{\text{3 generations (supplied rule)}}.
    \]

    Three distinct mechanisms contribute.

    \begin{enumerate}
      \item \textit{Lorentzian complexification fixes the spin group, on the supplied carrier.}
      The Weil module $\Vr$ of that carrier has a canonical symplectic structure; its automorphisms
      lift to $\Mp(2,\mathbb{R})$.
      The effective metric $g^{\mu\nu} = 2\eta^{\mu\nu}$ (Lorentzian) forces the Lie algebra
      complexification $\mathrm{mp}(2,\mathbb{R})_\mathbb{C} \simeq \mathfrak{sl}_2(\mathbb{C})$,
      whose Lorentzian integration is $\Spin(3,1) \simeq \SL(2,\mathbb{C})$.
      The spinor bundle $\Sbundle$ is the associated fundamental bundle.

      \item \textit{Tensor functors of $\Sbundle$ reproduce the electroweak gauge-sector data.}
      $\mathrm{Sym}^2(\mathbb{C}^2) \simeq \mathfrak{sl}_2(\mathbb{C})$: the symmetric sector gives
      the complex adjoint, from which the compact real form selects the $\SU(2)_L$ gauge sector.
      $\wedge^2(\mathbb{C}^2) \simeq \det(\mathbb{C}^2)$: the determinant line $L_Y$ supports the
      $\UU(1)_Y$ factor.
      No independent electroweak matter bundle is introduced --- and, by the calibration
      of~\cite{Beau2026nsr}, none is produced: the adjoint and the line are Lorentz-typed images
      of the same spinor factor, whereas a fermionic weak doublet requires a multiplicity factor
      $M_L \simeq \mathbb{C}^2$ commuting with the Lorentz action, which these functors do not
      supply.

      \item \textit{The projective endomorphism $\Epi$ enforces chirality, and constrains hypercharge
      on a supplied colour module.}
      The projected Dirac operator satisfies the Lichnerowicz-type identity
      \[
        D^2_{\Pi,g,A}
        = -(\nabla^{S,A})^2 + \frac{R}{4} + \frac{1}{2}\gamma^\mu\gamma^\nu F^a_{\mu\nu} T^a_R
        + \Epi.
      \]
      By the spinorial lift of BI parity, established in Q14~\cite{Beau2026q14}, $\Epi$ is left-admissible
      ($P_R \Epi P_R = 0$), giving the $V-A$ structure.
      That half carries no hypothesis beyond the supplied carrier.
      $\UU(1)_Y$ invariance of $\SPI[g,A,D_{\Pi,g,A}]$ imposes the anomaly-cancellation
      trace constraint $\mathrm{Tr}_{\Sbundle}(\gamma_5 Y A_\Pi(x)) = 0$ on the hypercharge weights.
      That constraint follows from symbol compatibility and left-admissibility, and carries no
      colour hypothesis.
      The supplied three-dimensional colour module enters at the next level, that of the rigidity of
      the weights: the anomaly sums are weighted by the representation dimension, so the colour
      multiplicity enters them.
      Selecting the Standard Model pattern from those constraints is a further step, under the
      additional premise that $L_Y$ carries the minimal integral normalisation compatible with the
      $\UU(1)_Y$ action, and then only up to an overall sign and rescaling; the cubic constraint
      vanishes on that pattern precisely at colour multiplicity three.
    \end{enumerate}

    \subsection{Q14: Fermionic matter and chirality from projective Dirac admissibility}
      \label{sec:q14}

      \textbf{Q14}~\cite{Beau2026q14} is the sole constituent paper.
      It is organised around three independent but mutually compatible structural results.

      \textit{Theorem~A: Spinorial electroweak bundle} (structural; relative to the supplied
      Heisenberg carrier).
      On that carrier, the Weil module $\Vr$ induces, via the metaplectic lift and the Lorentzian
      complexification forced by $g^{\mu\nu} = 2\eta^{\mu\nu}$, an admissible spinor bundle
      $\Sbundle$.
      Its tensor sectors satisfy:
      \[
        \mathrm{Sym}^2(\Sbundle) \simeq \adC(P_\Pi), \qquad \wedge^2(\Sbundle) = L_Y.
      \]
      After selection of the compact real form, these define the $\SU(2)_L \times \UU(1)_Y$
      gauge-sector bundle data without any input beyond that carrier.
      This is Q14's own construction from the tensor sectors of $\Sbundle$; it does not import a
      gauge-group identification from the gauge structure sub-programme, which supplies none.
      Calibration: these are adjoint and determinant data, typed by the Lorentz action on the
      same spinor factor.
      They do not constitute the independent fermionic weak doublet $M_L \simeq \mathbb{C}^2$,
      whose two deposited candidates are excluded by the exact obstructions
      of~\cite{Beau2026nsr}.

      \textit{Theorem~B: Chiral selection and hypercharge constraints}
      (structural; the two halves carry different hypotheses).
      The projected Dirac operator $\Dpi$ has the Lichnerowicz-type square above.
      By the spinorial lift of BI parity, established in Q14 (the antilinear $J_\Pi = HK$ maps
      right-chiral modes to non-admissible directions), $\Epi$ is left-admissible: $P_R \Epi P_R = 0$.
      This is the $V-A$ structure, and it carries no hypothesis beyond the supplied carrier, the
      axiomatic chain A1--A4 and the upstream BI parity datum.
      $\UU(1)_Y$ invariance of the projected spectral functional then imposes, at the $a_4$ level:
      \[
        \mathrm{Tr}_{\Sbundle}(\gamma_5 Y A_\Pi(x)) = 0 \quad \forall x \in M_{\mathrm{eff}},
      \]
      where $A_\Pi$ is the $\gamma_5$-weighted $a_4$ Seeley--DeWitt density of $D^2_{\Pi,g,A}$.
      This constraint is not postulated: it is the chiral consistency condition of the same $a_4$
      level that yields Yang--Mills dynamics in the bosonic variation.
      The hypercharge half carries further hypotheses, in two steps.
      First, the constraints are weighted by the representation dimension, so the colour
      multiplicity enters the sums: the rigidity of the weights $Y_R$ holds on a supplied
      three-dimensional colour module.
      Q14 does not analyse the constraint system at general multiplicity and does not claim that the
      constraints force three.
      Second, selecting the \emph{Standard Model pattern} from those constraints is a separate
      proposition with an additional premise: that the determinant line $L_Y = \wedge^2(\Sbundle)$
      carries the minimal integral normalisation compatible with the $\UU(1)_Y$ action on the
      projected spinorial sectors.
      Under that premise the pattern is selected only up to an overall sign and rescaling, and the
      cubic constraint vanishes on it precisely at colour multiplicity three.
      That premise is not discharged: Q14 lists the explicit connection between the determinant-line
      normalisation and the Standard Model pattern among its open directions, the anomaly
      constraints restricting the allowed weights while the normalisation fixes their scale.

      \textit{Theorem~C: Generation multiplicity} (structural; conditional on the supplied
      rank-three carrier).
      The saturation invariant $\sigma_c(n_3) = 3$, a supplied selection rule (O23 proves the
      three-dimensionality of the neutral sector of a supplied spinor carrier, Theorem~3.1),
      has two functorially distinct readings of the same rank-three admissible invariant.
      In the geometric branch (Note~2), it yields three effective spatial directions.
      In the spinorial branch, the spinorial multiplicity functor maps it to a gauge-singlet
      generation space:
      \[
        \Cgen \subset \ker(\mathrm{ad}_{\SU(2)} \oplus Y).
      \]
      The identity $\mathbb{R}^3_{\mathrm{space}} \neq C^3_{\mathrm{gen}}$ is established
      explicitly (Q14 §5): the spatial and generation spaces are distinct functorial images of the
      same admissible invariant.
      Three generations follow as a gauge-singlet factor, on that supplied rank-three carrier.
      A qualitative mechanism for their splitting is identified in Q14 \S6 (summarised below); the
      explicit $\Epi$ and the amplitude of the splitting remain open.

      \textit{Dynamic generation lifting} (localisation and A4 matching).
      The internal structure of $\Cgen$ is probed by the spectrum of $\Epi^2$ (Q14 \S6).
      A static $J_\Pi$-real, weight-preserving restriction of $\Epi^2$ has a degenerate outer pair:
      the two light generations cannot be split statically (static obstruction).
      The admissible lifts removing this degeneracy form a two-dimensional $J_\Pi$-odd sector, the
      real-split direction being the weight generator $J_3$ and the mixing direction a doublet
      rotation.
      The generation split is not a finite-front observable in the symmetric sense: the raw finite front
      remains $J_\Pi$-equivariant and gives $u_{\mathrm{fin}}=0$~\cite{Beau2026prs}.
      After Lorentzian complexification, however, the chiral projector makes it meaningful to read a
      $J_\Pi$-odd frontier cocycle, and the residual automorphism is the central Weil lift
      $R_b=W(-I)=\mathrm{FT}^2$, which commutes with $\gamma_5$: it is blind to chirality and does not

      The remaining amplitude is fixed at A4 by matching the discrete chiral-frontier normalisation

      $\mu_\chi^2=\partial_s^2\mathcal B(0)$~\cite{Beau2026prs}, neither ingredient reducing to the other.

      The quark bundle is therefore a conditional output of the sub-programme.

    \subsection{Angular Amplitude Reduction: the $J_3$ split as oriented symplectic
    area~\cite{Beau2026aar}}
      This note fixes the numerical observable for the absolute normalisation of the angular generation
      split before any Weil--BFS computation.
      A Baker--Campbell--Hausdorff reduction identifies the per-step $J_\Pi$-odd $J_3$ component of the
      metaplectic cascade with the oriented symplectic area swept per step --- the central,
      Carnot-degree-2 increment of $\Heis_3$ --- and not with a free shear parameter.
      The quantity to measure is the accumulated oriented $J_3$-odd area along the cascade, normalised by
      the projected capacity.
      A direct evaluation on symmetric BFS shells returns $\Theta_{\mathrm{raw}}=0$ identically: the
      symmetric capacity data fix the radial sector but cannot select an oriented angular branch, so the
      orientation prescription is an irreducible input prior to the normalisation $\mathcal N_A$.
      A subsequent capacity audit on the real Weil--BFS pipeline closes the angular onset coefficient: the directed
      outgoing-frontier mean $\langle|\Delta A_c|\rangle_{\partial^+}$ is a $q$-independent exact rational sequence
      (identical across $q\in\{61,101,151,211,307\}$, by integer arithmetic, no fit) with saturation onset exactly
      $\tfrac13$, so the maximal-locking angular bound is $\theta_{\max}=2\pi/(3q)$.
      The onset is exact for the standard $\{\pm X,\pm Y\}$ campaign but generator-set dependent --- its inverse
      $1/\mathrm{onset}=3$ is the outgoing graph valence, not $\dim\mathrm{Sym}^2$ --- so only $\varepsilon$ and the
      level ratio $R=\tfrac32$ are generator-independent.
      This closes the angular coefficient, not the dictionary value: since $\theta_{\max}\to0$ with $q$, reaching
      $\varepsilon=1/10$ would require the geometric $\mathrm{Sym}^2$ normalisation $\mathcal N_A^{\mathrm{geom}}=3q/(20\pi)$,
      which neither the capacity nor the $\mathrm{Sym}^2$ data supply, leaving $\varepsilon$ gated by the ADE
      datum~\cite{Beau2026aog}.

        The Yukawa Lagrangian $\mathcal{L}_Y$ is identified as arising from the trace

        \item \textit{An admissible colour sector.}
        No colour module is derived, so the quark bundle and the hypercharge selection both rest on

        The open problem is therefore the construction of an admissible colour sector itself, not
        the extension of an established one beyond the standard Cayley graph.

        \item \textit{The fermionic weak multiplicity $M_L$ and the parallel-channel frontier.}
        The independent weak doublet carrier $M_L \simeq \mathbb{C}^2$ commuting with the
        Lorentz action is not derived by the corpus, and its two deposited candidates are now
        excluded exactly~\cite{Beau2026nsr}: the internal Hecke operator doublets have no

        the protected sector.
        These are two new ingredients --- two new axioms, or one new axiom supplying both; the
        frontier is explicitly an extension question, not a derivation question, and an
        independent new multiplicity carrier outside the deposited corpus remains a distinct,
        unconstrained extension architecture.

        \item \textit{Neutrino identity as a projective test case.}
        The weak flavour basis and the mass propagation basis are not aligned, and in the present framework
        there is no structural reason for the weak flavour basis to diagonalize the projected spectral mass
        residue: basis misalignment is the generic situation, and alignment requires an additional projective
        stabiliser.
        This reverses the usual reading of mixing: the large PMNS mixing is the unfixed default, in line with
        the anarchy hypothesis~\cite{HallMurayamaWeiner2000}, while the near-diagonality of the CKM matrix
        becomes the non-trivial feature to be explained, requiring the charged projection channels to pin the
        up-type and down-type mass bases to a common projective frame.
        The full development --- the neutral-meson comparison, the ontological status of elementary
        misalignment~\cite{Hobart2026OnticTypes}, the Dirac/Majorana reading, and the structural test relating
        mixing magnitude to the number of identity-stabilising projection channels --- is carried by the
        companion note~\cite{Beau2026npi}.
      \end{enumerate}

  \section{Open Deliverables}
    \label{sec:open}

    The derivation of the spinorial BI lift, formerly the first open deliverable, is now
    established as a theorem in Q14~\cite{Beau2026q14}: the antilinear lift $J_\Pi = HK$ dresses
    the BI parity by the weight grading.
    This removes the lift as a technical condition on Theorems~B and~C.
    It does not make them unconditional: Theorem~C still carries the supplied rank-three carrier,
    the hypercharge weight rigidity of Theorem~B still carries the supplied colour module, and the
    selection of the Standard Model pattern carries in addition the minimal integral normalisation
    of $L_Y$, whose connection to that pattern Q14 keeps among its own open directions.
    Two open deliverables remain.

    \textbf{Open deliverable 1: Explicit $\Epi$ and the Yukawa sector.}
    The mass spectrum of charged leptons and quarks, and the CKM/PMNS mixing matrices,
    require the explicit spectral construction of $\Epi$ as an operator on the admissible
    Hilbert space.
    This requires integrating the projected Yukawa Lagrangian $\mathcal{L}_Y$ and
    extracting the mass matrix from the eigenvalues of $\Epi^2$.
    This is the primary open problem of the fermionic sector.
    The structural form of $\Epi$ is now fixed as the Schur complement of the eliminated spinorial
    block~\cite{Beau2026prs};
    the open part is its explicit Lorentzian block $1-P(s)$ along the $J_\Pi$-odd modulus.
    The symmetric finite front is $J_\Pi$-equivariant and gives $u_{\mathrm{fin}}=0$, but the discrete
    chiral-frontier normalisation $\mathcal N_A$ survives the operator obstruction, since the residual
    reflection $R_b=\mathrm{FT}^2$ is blind to chirality ($[\gamma_5,R_b]=0$)~\cite{Beau2026q11of}, and is fixed
    maximally, $\mathcal N_A=\theta_{\max}$, in the inherited central-phase lift~\cite{Beau2026cho}.
    The split amplitude is therefore fixed at A4 by matching this discrete normalisation with the
    Lorentzian Born--Infeld curvature $\mu_\chi^2=\partial_s^2\mathcal B(0)$, neither ingredient reducing
    to the other:
    \[
      |u| = \mathrm{A4Match}\!\left(\mathcal N_A,\,\mu_\chi^2\right).
    \]
    The operator side of this matching is no longer conditional: by~\cite{Beau2026prs} the Schur transversality is
    closed in the present Lorentzian spin stratum, the projected commutator
    $[\partial_s P|_0,J_\Pi]\big|_{\mathrm{span}(e_+,e_-)}$ having the exact metaplectic opening
    $\alpha(t,s)=ts\,r/\sinh r\not\equiv0$ with timelike Clifford symbol off the zero-mode locus.
    In the Schur-transverse branch the A4 chiral polarisation is electric, hence
    $\mathcal P_{\mu\nu}\mathcal P^{\mu\nu}<0$, $\mu_\chi^2<0$, and the split opens spontaneously~\cite{Beau2026bim};
    the genus is determined.
    What remains open is the split value $|u|$ and the Yukawa sector downstream of $u$.
    On the corpus-derived real cascade the A4 companion resolves the boundary-versus-interior selection in favour of
    saturation: the transverse (spin-two) cubic response of $\mathcal P\cdot\mathcal R_\chi$ vanishes path-invariantly at
    $\gamma=0$, so the derived amplitude mechanism is Born--Infeld saturation, and the sixth-order interior lock is doubly
    conditional --- on a non-derived complex phase and a non-prescribed ordering --- hence not concluded~\cite{Beau2026bim}.
    The value $|u|$ itself is not a parameter-free prediction: it is dictionary-bound through the chiral-frontier
    normalisation $\mathcal N_A$, the angular analogue of the radial scale $\kappa=5/12$ of the exit-deficit

    three-level deficit structure to the external ADE ratio of a selected case~\cite{Beau2026a4}.
    The genuine first-principles front for the value is therefore upstream and structural, not a numerical amplitude: the
    ADE case selection and the level-to-generation map, the derivation of the projective-resolution growth and the cascade
    exponent $\beta$, and the derivation of the transfer
    constant $N_{\mathrm{casc}}$ that fixes $\mathcal N_A$~\cite{Beau2026q14}.
    No structural bound on $\beta$ is available: the Born--Infeld flux constraint together with the
    Cheeger inequality for Ramanujan--LPS graphs does not yield one, and repairing that route needs
    structural choices absent from the Born--Infeld axiom itself~\cite{Beau2026a8}.
    Deriving the projective-resolution growth $\Lambda_{\mathrm{proj}}(n)$ from first principles is
    the central open problem of that front~\cite{Beau2026a4}.
    What remains quantitative is therefore these upstream dictionary inputs, the integral normalisation of
    $\mathcal{L}_Y$, and generation mixing via the complex metaplectic phase.

    \paragraph{Charged-lepton unit-dispersion target.}
    The phenomenological target for the open charged-lepton masses is stated most sharply not as a mass ratio but
    as a scale-free shape invariant: the Koide relation is equivalent to unit relative dispersion of the
    square-root mass vector $r_\ell = (\sqrt{m_e}, \sqrt{m_\mu}, \sqrt{m_\tau})$, since
    $Q_\ell = (1 + \mathrm{CV}(r_\ell)^2)/3$, so that $Q_\ell = 2/3 \iff \mathrm{CV}(r_\ell) = 1$
    (standard deviation equal to mean).
    The projected generation ladder $(1, \tfrac12 + u, \tfrac12 - u)$ has bounded dispersion,
    $\mathrm{CV} \le 1/\sqrt2$ and $Q \le 1/2$, and therefore cannot by itself reach the Koide value; the dispersion
    constraint and the concrete hierarchy (the placement of one generation near a node of the square-root profile)
    separate.
    KUD records this target invariant, the open maximum-entropy test, and the scoped linear self-adjoint
    determinant no-go described above~\cite{Beau2026kud}.

    The Yukawa sector now splits into two sub-fronts.
    The Yukawa line is closed: the determinant line $L_Y = \wedge^2(\Sbundle)$ is the unique functorial line of the
    carrier, invisible to the $\SU(2)_L$ adjoint sector and carrying the abelian weight, and the generation-level
    assignment is fixed by the spectral levels of
    $\Epi^2|_{\Cgen} = \mathrm{diag}(1, \tfrac12 + u, \tfrac12 - u)$, the central direction being lightest and the largest
    exit deficit heaviest~\cite{Beau2026pyl}.
    The Yukawa operator $Y_\Pi: \mathcal{S}_{L,\Pi} \otimes L_Y \to \mathcal{S}_{R,\Pi}$ in turn splits.
    Its hermitian square is closed: the Schur-residue sector fixes the squared observable
    $H_\Pi := Y_\Pi^\dagger Y_\Pi$, with $\mathrm{Spec}(H_\Pi)|_{\Cgen} = \lambda_Y^2\,\{1, \tfrac12 + u, \tfrac12 - u\}$,
    so $\Epi^2|_{\Cgen}$ determines the squared Yukawa levels, not the morphism~\cite{Beau2026pyo}.
    What stays open is the chiral polar factor $U_\Pi$ in $Y_\Pi = U_\Pi H_\Pi^{1/2}$ --- a unitary chiral map on the
    support of $H_\Pi$ --- which carries the chiral orientation and the complex mixing phase, together with the separate
    positive Yukawa norm $\lambda_Y$ that scales $H_\Pi$ (a unitary cannot carry it), and hence the absolute masses and

    $\Epi^2$ is blind to $U_\Pi$: the squared residue can close $H_\Pi = Y_\Pi^\dagger Y_\Pi$ but cannot close $Y_\Pi$

    The polar factor is not canonical but a rephasing class, $U_\Pi \sim V_R\,U_\Pi\,V_L^{-1}$ under the admissible

    On the derived real cascade that transverse channel is absent ($v = 0$), so $[U_\Pi] = [I]$ and no physical mixing

    are real and the only inherited complex phase is central~\cite{Beau2026pyo}.

  \section{Conclusion}
    \label{sec:conclusion}

    The fermionic matter sub-programme shows that fermions, chirality and three generations need
    not be added to the Cosmochrony framework as external fields, once a Heisenberg carrier is
    supplied.
    On that carrier they are the spinorial face of the Weil module $\Vr$, revealed through the
    Lorentzian complexification of its metaplectic structure.
    The carrier is supplied rather than selected by the admissibility axioms, and everything below
    inherits that condition.

    The electroweak gauge-sector data of $\SU(2)_L \times \UU(1)_Y$ arise from the tensor
    functors of the admissible spinor bundle $\Sbundle$ (Theorem~A, no further hypothesis); the
    independent fermionic weak doublet is a distinct object, not produced by those functors
    and now excluded at the operator and history strata by the companion no-go
    note~\cite{Beau2026nsr}.
    The $V-A$ chiral structure arises from the projective endomorphism
    $\Epi$ (Theorem~B, no further hypothesis).
    The anomaly-cancellation trace constrains the hypercharge weights with no colour hypothesis;
    their rigidity is what carries a supplied three-dimensional colour module.
    Selecting the Standard Model pattern among them takes a further premise, the minimal integral
    normalisation of the determinant line $L_Y$, holds only up to an overall sign and rescaling, and
    is where the cubic constraint vanishes at colour multiplicity three; Q14 leaves the explicit
    connection between that normalisation and the pattern open.
    Three generations arise from the spinorial multiplicity reading of $\sigma_c(n_3) = 3$
    (Theorem~C, conditional on the supplied rank-three selection rule of O23).
    The colour-coupled quark sector follows by tensoring with $V_{\mathrm{color}}$, and carries that
    same supplied module: no admissible colour sector is derived~\cite{Beau2026a35}.
    The matter-sector weak doublet stands as the explicitly architectural frontier of the
    sub-programme: its realisation through the deposited operator or history structures
    requires a parallel substrate channel with an oriented central/Weyl datum --- two new
    ingredients, or one new axiom supplying both, that no deposited principle provides ---
    while an independent new multiplicity carrier remains a distinct extension architecture
    (Section~\ref{sec:open-problems}).

    The inter-generation splitting is localised: statically obstructed, living in a two-dimensional
    $J_\Pi$-odd lift sector, and vanishing on the symmetric finite front ($u_{\mathrm{fin}}=0$,
    $J_\Pi$-equivariant).
    Its operator obstruction is resolved, the residual reflection $R_b=\mathrm{FT}^2$ being blind to
    chirality ($[\gamma_5,R_b]=0$), and its amplitude mechanism is the derived Born--Infeld
    saturation~\cite{Beau2026bim}.
    The remaining open components are the explicit Lorentzian $\Epi$, whose eliminated-block jet $1-P(s)$ is structurally
    constrained and whose chiral generator is identified as the transported equivariance defect rate
    $[\Pi_{J_\Pi\text{-odd}}\dot Q(0)]_{LL}=\tfrac12\partial_s\Delta_\chi(P)|_0$ in the companion note~\cite{Beau2026ebj}
    (reducing the split value to the single normalisation target $\partial_s\Delta_\chi(P)|_0$); the split value, which is
    dictionary-bound and whose first-principles derivation is upstream and structural (the ADE case selection and
    level-to-generation map, the cascade exponent $\beta$, and the transfer constant $N_{\mathrm{casc}}$); and the
    derivation of the Yukawa sector and mass spectrum.

    The local operator chain is closed in the eliminated-block and saturation companions~\cite{Beau2026ebj, Beau2026bim}:
    the split carrier is the transported chiral defect rate, and the contact split factorises as
    \[
      u_\partial = \sqrt{2}\,\sigma\,s_*\,\frac{T_{J_3}(\partial_s\Delta_\chi(P)|_0)}{\langle J_3, J_3\rangle},
    \]
    where the Born--Infeld even-block factor $\sqrt{2}\,\sigma$ is structural, $s_*$ is the radial A4 contact, and $T_{J_3}$
    is the oriented angular observable whose normalised evaluation is dictionary-bound through $\mathcal{N}_A$.
    Thus the remaining magnitude problem is upstream, not local to the eliminated block.

    The mass-sector frontier now separates cleanly into three layers.
    The present stratum derives the diagonal generation split: the angular Weil--BFS coefficient is closed in the
    standard campaign ($\theta_{\max}=2\pi/(3q)$), and the Schur transport gives $u\neq0$~\cite{Beau2026aar, Beau2026prs}.
    It does not derive polar mixing: the non-central metaplectic coefficients are real at this stratum, so the chiral
    polar class collapses to $[U_\Pi]=[I]$~\cite{Beau2026pyo}.
    Finally, the dictionary value $\varepsilon=1/10$ is not a capacity fit and is not fixed by $\mathrm{Sym}^2(\mathbb{C}^2)$
    alone; after the capacity and $\mathrm{Sym}^2$ normalisations are separated, the remaining datum is the
    ADE / type-rigidity ratio $R=3/2$ --- equivalently the prime $5$, or the $\sqrt{5}$-gate --- whose selection over the
    neutral order-four convention ($R=\tfrac53$, $\varepsilon=\tfrac18$) is the single residual lock~\cite{Beau2026aog}.

    The neutrino sector provides a particularly sharp interpretive test case: if particle identity is projective
    rather than primitive, then PMNS-like misalignment is the generic neutral case, while CKM near-alignment is
    the structure requiring explanation (Section~\ref{sec:open-problems}).
