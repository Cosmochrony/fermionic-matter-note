  \begin{abstract}
    The fermionic matter sub-programme of the Cosmochrony corpus addresses a single
    central question: where do fermions, chirality, hypercharge, and three generations come
    from?
    The answer is that they are not postulated as external fields: they arise as the spinorial
    face of the admissible Weil module $\Vr$, once its metaplectic Lie-algebraic structure is
    complexified by the Lorentzian metric established in the geometric branch.
    The structural chain is:
    \[
      \Pi \Rightarrow F_n \simeq \Vr \Rightarrow \Mp(2,\mathbb{R})
      \Rightarrow \mathrm{mp}(2,\mathbb{R})_\mathbb{C} \simeq \mathfrak{sl}_2(\mathbb{C})
      \rightsquigarrow \Spin(3,1) \simeq \SL(2,\mathbb{C})
      \Rightarrow \Sbundle.
    \]
    Three modular structural results are established in Q14: (A)~the tensor functors of the
    admissible spinor bundle $\Sbundle$ produce the gauge-sector data of
    $\SU(2)_L \times \UU(1)_Y$ --- the complex adjoint from $\mathrm{Sym}^2(\Sbundle)$ and the
    hypercharge line from $\wedge^2(\Sbundle)$ --- these being Lorentz-typed bundle data, not an
    independent fermionic weak doublet carrier; (B)~the projected Dirac operator $\Dpi$ contains a
    canonical projective endomorphism $\Epi$ that enforces the $V-A$ chiral structure
    (established via the spinorial BI lift theorem of Q14) and whose $\gamma_5$-weighted $a_4$ coefficient imposes
    anomaly-cancellation constraints making hypercharge a spectral datum; (C)~the saturation
    invariant $\sigma_c(n_3) = 3$ has a spinorial multiplicity reading yielding a gauge-singlet
    three-generation factor $\Cgen$.
    The colour-coupled quark sector is unconditional at the pointwise level
    ($[\mathrm{H\text{-}color}]_{\mathrm{pointwise}}$ established in O31).
    The structural form of $\Epi$ is now fixed as a Schur complement of the eliminated spinorial block, and the
    generation-split amplitude mechanism is derived as Born--Infeld saturation in the companion note~\cite{Beau2026bim};
    the open deliverables are its explicit Lorentzian block and the Yukawa sector, while the split value itself is
    dictionary-bound, its first-principles derivation relocated upstream to the ADE case selection and the cascade exponent.
    A dedicated no-go note~\cite{Beau2026nsr} now closes the two deposited candidates for the independent fermionic weak
    multiplicity $M_L \simeq \mathbb{C}^2$ (the internal operator doublets and the erased history fibres) through exact
    obstructions culminating in the no-sequential-re-entry theorem; the matter-sector weak doublet is therefore an open
    frontier requiring a parallel substrate channel, stated as an explicit extension rather than a derivation.
  \end{abstract}

  \section{Motivation and Central Question}
    \label{sec:motivation}

    The fermionic matter sub-programme answers the following question.

    \medskip
    \noindent\textit{Central question.}
    The bosonic spectral stratification — gravity at $a_2$, Yang--Mills at $a_4$ --- derives
    the bosonic sector of the Standard Model from the same admissible spectral functional.
    Fermions, chirality, hypercharge assignments, and the three-generation structure are not
    covered by the bosonic synthesis.
    \textit{Do these fermionic structures also arise as forced consequences of the admissible
    Weil fibre, or must they be introduced as additional postulates?}

    \medskip
    The answer established in Q14~\cite{Beau2026q14} is stratified, not uniform.
    Fermions arise as the spinorial face of the Weil module $\Vr$ after Lorentzian
    complexification of its metaplectic lift: spinoriality is forced.
    Chirality and the $V-A$ structure arise from the projective endomorphism $\Epi$ of the
    projected Dirac operator, via the spinorial lift of BI parity established in Q14: the
    chiral selection is forced.
    Hypercharge is not a free parameter: it is constrained by spectral anomaly cancellation.
    Three generations arise, conditionally on the supplied rank-three carrier, from the
    spinorial multiplicity reading of the same invariant $\sigma_c(n_3) = 3$ that yields the
    three spatial directions in the geometric branch; the threshold is a supplied selection
    rule (O23 proves the three-dimensionality of the neutral sector of a supplied spinor
    carrier, Theorem~3.1; the carrier selection and the identification of $\Sigma_c$ with
    that dimension are open).
    What is NOT forced at the current stratum is the independent fermionic weak doublet: the
    tensor functors of $\Sbundle$ produce gauge-sector (adjoint and determinant) data typed by
    the Lorentz action, and the Lorentz commutant of the irreducible Weyl factor is scalar, so
    no internal non-abelian factor acts on the same spinor factor.
    The two deposited candidates for the missing multiplicity carrier $M_L \simeq \mathbb{C}^2$
    --- the internal Hecke operator doublets and the erased history fibres --- are closed by
    exact obstructions in the companion no-go note~\cite{Beau2026nsr}, whose central result is
    the no-sequential-re-entry theorem: information erased by the projective channel cannot be
    recovered by any tower of subsequent projections.
    The matter-sector weak doublet is therefore an explicitly open frontier, realisable only
    through a parallel substrate channel with an oriented central/Weyl datum --- a stated
    architectural extension, not a consequence of the deposited corpus.

    \medskip
    This sub-programme is the first paper of the Q-series that depends simultaneously on all
    four layers of the programme: the admissible Weil fibre structure (Notes~1 and~5), the
    closed Lorentzian geometry (Note~2), the gauge bundle (Note~3), and the gauge--gravity
    synthesis (Q13~\cite{Beau2026q13}, to be summarised in Presentation Note~8).

  \section{Logical Chain of the Sub-Programme}
    \label{sec:chain}

    The sub-programme is organised around the following structural chain.

    \begin{equation}
      \label{eq:chain}
      \Pi \Rightarrow F_n \simeq \Vr
      \;\Rightarrow\;
      \Mp(2,\mathbb{R})
      \;\Rightarrow\;
      \mathrm{mp}(2,\mathbb{R})_\mathbb{C} \simeq \mathfrak{sl}_2(\mathbb{C})
      \;\rightsquigarrow\;
      \Spin(3,1) \simeq \SL(2,\mathbb{C})
      \;\Rightarrow\;

    \[
      \;\Rightarrow\;
      \underbrace{\mathrm{Sym}^2(\Sbundle) \Rightarrow \adC(P_\Pi)}_{\SU(2)_L}
      \;,\quad
      \underbrace{\wedge^2(\Sbundle) = L_Y}_{\UU(1)_Y}
      \;,\quad
      \underbrace{\Epi \text{ left-admissible}}_{V-A}
      \;,\quad
      \underbrace{\sigma_c(n_3)=3 \to \Cgen}_{\text{3 generations (supplied rule)}}.
    \]

    Three distinct mechanisms contribute.

    \begin{enumerate}
      \item \textit{Lorentzian complexification forces the spin group.}
      The Weil module $\Vr$ carries a canonical symplectic structure; its automorphisms lift to
      $\Mp(2,\mathbb{R})$.
      The effective metric $g^{\mu\nu} = 2\eta^{\mu\nu}$ (Lorentzian) forces the Lie algebra

      The spinor bundle $\Sbundle$ is the associated fundamental bundle.

      \item \textit{Tensor functors of $\Sbundle$ reproduce the electroweak gauge-sector data.}
      $\mathrm{Sym}^2(\mathbb{C}^2) \simeq \mathfrak{sl}_2(\mathbb{C})$: the symmetric sector gives
      the complex adjoint, from which the compact real form selects the $\SU(2)_L$ gauge sector.
      $\wedge^2(\mathbb{C}^2) \simeq \det(\mathbb{C}^2)$: the determinant line $L_Y$ supports the
      $\UU(1)_Y$ factor.
      No independent electroweak matter bundle is introduced --- and, by the calibration
      of~\cite{Beau2026nsr}, none is produced: the adjoint and the line are Lorentz-typed images
      of the same spinor factor, whereas a fermionic weak doublet requires a multiplicity factor
      $M_L \simeq \mathbb{C}^2$ commuting with the Lorentz action, which these functors do not
      supply.

      \item \textit{The projective endomorphism $\Epi$ enforces chirality and hypercharge.}

        D^2_{\Pi,g,A}
        = -(\nabla^{S,A})^2 + \frac{R}{4} + \frac{1}{2}\gamma^\mu\gamma^\nu F^a_{\mu\nu} T^a_R

      By the spinorial lift of BI parity, established in Q14~\cite{Beau2026q14}, $\Epi$ is left-admissible
      ($P_R \Epi P_R = 0$), giving the $V-A$ structure.
      $\UU(1)_Y$ invariance of $\SPI[g,A,D_{\Pi,g,A}]$ imposes the anomaly-cancellation

    \end{enumerate}

  \section{Conclusion}
    \label{sec:conclusion}

    The fermionic matter sub-programme establishes that fermions, chirality, hypercharge,
    and three generations are not external additions to the Cosmochrony framework.
    They are the spinorial face of the admissible Weil module $\Vr$, revealed through the
    Lorentzian complexification of its metaplectic structure.

    The electroweak gauge-sector data of $\SU(2)_L \times \UU(1)_Y$ arise from the tensor
    functors of the admissible spinor bundle $\Sbundle$ (Theorem~A, unconditional); the
    independent fermionic weak doublet is a distinct object, not produced by those functors
    and now excluded at the operator and history strata by the companion no-go
    note~\cite{Beau2026nsr}.
    The $V-A$ chiral structure and hypercharge rigidity arise from the projective endomorphism
    $\Epi$ (Theorem~B, unconditional).
    Three generations arise from the spinorial multiplicity reading of $\sigma_c(n_3) = 3$
    (Theorem~C, conditional on the supplied rank-three selection rule of O23).
    The colour-coupled quark sector follows by tensoring with $V_{\mathrm{color}}$, unconditional
    at the pointwise level (O31).
    The matter-sector weak doublet stands as the explicitly architectural frontier of the
    sub-programme: its realisation through the deposited operator or history structures
    requires a parallel substrate channel with an oriented central/Weyl datum --- two new
    ingredients, or one new axiom supplying both, that no deposited principle provides ---
    while an independent new multiplicity carrier remains a distinct extension architecture

    The inter-generation splitting is localised: statically obstructed, living in a two-dimensional
    $J_\Pi$-odd lift sector, and vanishing on the symmetric finite front ($u_{\mathrm{fin}}=0$,
    $J_\Pi$-equivariant).
    Its operator obstruction is resolved, the residual reflection $R_b=\mathrm{FT}^2$ being blind to
