  \begin{abstract}
    The fermionic matter sub-programme of the Cosmochrony corpus asks where fermions, chirality, hypercharge,
    and three generations could come from in the admissible Weil fibre.
    The answer is layered, and each layer names what it needs.
    On a \emph{supplied} Heisenberg carrier, given a real metaplectic model and a supplied doublet, the algebra
    is proved and uses no metric: $\mathrm{mp}(2,\mathbb{R})_\mathbb{C} \simeq \mathfrak{sl}_2(\mathbb{C})$, the
    symmetric square of the doublet is the adjoint, its exterior square the determinant line.
    The admissibility axioms do not select that carrier, and every result below inherits it.
    Two physical readings of this algebra are distinct hypotheses, supplied by no source and excluded by none.
    [H-Spin] identifies the $\SL(2,\mathbb{C})$ of the doublet with the spin group of a four-dimensional
    Lorentzian co-metric on the effective base; under it $\mathrm{Sym}^2(S_L)$ is the Lorentz sector,
    $\wedge^2(S_L)$ a trivial line, and the projective endomorphism $\Epi$ of the projected Dirac operator is
    left-admissible; left-admissibility is the definition of the orientation-compatible branch, an input, given the
    Born--Infeld datum (H1)--(H2) of O30.
    [H-Weak] supplies a distinct rank-two weak factor $\Ew$ with structure group $\UU(2)$: $\su(2)_L$ sits in
    $\mathrm{Sym}^2(\Ew)$, the hypercharge line is $L_Y := \wedge^2(\Ew)$, and the $V-A$ structure is its content.
    The anomaly constraints on the hypercharge weights need both hypotheses.
    Lorentz chirality and weak chiral selection are different statements; the second is not derived from the first.
    The rigidity of the hypercharge weights carries, beyond the two hypotheses, a supplied colour module and the
    admissible matter decomposition, and
    selecting the Standard Model pattern carries in addition the minimal integral normalisation of $L_Y$.
    The saturation invariant $\sigma_c(n_3) = 3$, a supplied rule, gives conditionally a gauge-singlet
    three-generation factor $\Cgen$; the generation sector is a set of model operators on a distinct supplied
    doublet, their reading as restrictions of $\Epi^2$, named [H-Res], is not supplied, the level-to-generation map
    is not established, and the split value is a matching value.
    O31 is a withdrawal notice: the co-admissibility of the colour profiles, the uniqueness of $\SU(3)$, the
    arithmetic criterion for colour triplets and every gauge-factor derivation are withdrawn, so no admissible colour
    sector in that sense is established.
    A dedicated no-go note (NSR) excludes the two deposited candidates for an independent fermionic weak
    multiplicity $M_L \simeq \mathbb{C}^2$.
    NSR's finite-stratum obstructions are unconditional (not conditional on [H-Spin]); its Lorentz-lock input is
    imported from Q14, where the spinor reading of the carrier is under [H-Spin].
    This note reads $\Ew$ as the role such a multiplicity would play, so that
    $\Ew$ remains an explicit architectural extension.
    \emph{Interpretive outlook (a reading, not a result).}
    Chirality is where the internal algebra of the admissible fibre must meet spacetime geometry and the weak
    interaction at once; the two hypotheses locate that meeting point, and turn the question of why weak
    interactions are left-handed into the question of how the metaplectic symmetry of the fibre is joined to Lorentz
    frames and to a separate internal doublet.
  \end{abstract}

  \section{Motivation and Central Question}
    \label{sec:motivation}

    \paragraph{Convention for \texorpdfstring{$J_\Pi$}{J-Pi}.}
    The notation $J_\Pi$ is interpreted on the carrier specified in each statement.
    On the generation carrier $\Cgen$, it denotes the internal antilinear parity of Q14 Section~6.
    In the spinorial statements, including the EBJ subsection and the relation
    $R_b=\varphi\circ J_\Pi^{-1}$, it denotes the chirality-reversing spinorial parity:
    $J_\Pi\gamma_5=-\gamma_5J_\Pi$, under the hypotheses of the spinorial lift.
    This is the convention used in Q11-OF and CHO~\cite{Beau2026q11of, Beau2026cho}.
    The internal parity on $\Cgen$ alone does not supply a spinorial lift or this chirality relation.

    The fermionic matter sub-programme answers the following question.

    \medskip
    \noindent\textit{Central question.}
    The bosonic spectral stratification --- gravity at $a_2$, Yang--Mills at $a_4$ --- derives
    the bosonic sector of the Standard Model from the same admissible spectral functional.
    Fermions, chirality, hypercharge assignments, and the three-generation structure are not
    covered by the bosonic synthesis.
    \textit{Do these fermionic structures also arise as forced consequences of the admissible
    Weil fibre, or must they be introduced as additional postulates?}

    \medskip
    The answer given in Q14~\cite{Beau2026q14} is stratified, not uniform.
    It is stated throughout relative to a \emph{supplied} Heisenberg carrier and a fixed non-trivial
    central character: the identification $F_n \simeq \Vr$ is not selected by the admissibility
    axioms, which force irreducibility and non-commutation but neither make the commutator central
    nor single out a finite Heisenberg group, an explicit $\mathfrak{S}_3$ countermodel satisfying
    the extracted carrier contract~\cite{Beau2026m, Beau2026HeisenbergStructure}.
    On that carrier the finite Weil module acts through $\SL(2,\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z})$, so a real metaplectic
    model and a doublet carrying its Lie algebra are supplied model data.
    Given them, the algebra is proved and needs no metric: $\mathrm{mp}(2,\mathbb{R})_\mathbb{C} \simeq
    \mathfrak{sl}_2(\mathbb{C})$, the symmetric square of the doublet is the adjoint and its exterior square the
    determinant line.
    Two physical readings of this algebra are separate hypotheses, each a missing identification and neither
    excluded by any result.
    \HSpin{} (Hypothesis~2.2 of Q14) identifies the $\SL(2,\mathbb{C})$ acting on the doublet with the spin group of
    a four-dimensional Lorentzian co-metric on the effective base, and no source supplies it: the geometric branch
    supplies only a rank-three degenerate co-metric, under its hypotheses, and nothing relates the metaplectic
    action to its
    frames.
    \HWeak{} (Hypothesis~2.3 of Q14) supplies a distinct rank-two weak factor $\Ew$ with structure group $\UU(2)$,
    which Q14 does not construct: by Schur's lemma a weak action commuting with Lorentz transformations cannot live
    on the doublet that carries the spin reading.
    Under \HSpin{}, spinoriality is a statement about spacetime spinors, $\mathrm{Sym}^2(S_L)$ is the Lorentz sector,
    and $\wedge^2(S_L)$ is a trivial line carrying no hypercharge.
    Under \HWeak{}, the same tensor functors applied to $\Ew$ give the weak adjoint, whose compact real form is
    $\su(2)_L$, and the determinant line $L_Y := \wedge^2(\Ew)$, which carries $\UU(1)_Y$ non-trivially only because
    the structure group of $\Ew$ is $\UU(2)$.
    Chirality splits in two.
    Lorentz chirality is the statement that the projective endomorphism $\Epi$ of the projected Dirac operator is
    left-admissible, $P_R \Epi P_R = 0$: under \HSpin{}, the Born--Infeld datum (H1)--(H2) of O30 and the
    orientation-compatible branch, which is an input.
    The weak chiral selection, the $V-A$ structure, is the representation content of \HWeak{}, and Q14 does not
    derive it from left-admissibility.
    Hypercharge does not stand on the same footing, and it separates into two steps.
    The anomaly-cancellation constraints, which require both hypotheses, are weighted by the representation
    dimension, so the rigidity of the hypercharge weights carries a supplied three-dimensional colour module as a
    hypothesis; Q14 neither analyses the constraint system at general multiplicity nor claims that
    the constraints force three.
    Selecting the Standard Model pattern from those constraints needs a further premise: that the
    determinant line $L_Y = \wedge^2(\Ew)$ carries the minimal integral normalisation
    compatible with the $\UU(1)_Y$ action on the projected spinorial sectors.
    Under that premise, and on the Standard Model content without a right-handed neutrino and with $Y_e \neq 0$, the
    pattern is selected only up to an overall sign, rescaling and the exchange of the labels $u_R$ and $d_R$, the
    cubic constraint vanishing on it precisely at colour multiplicity three.
    The constraints are the gauge-curvature ones only: the $\Epi$-dependent traces are not analysed in Q14.
    Q14 records the explicit connection of that normalisation to the pattern as an open direction:
    the anomaly constraints restrict the allowed weights, while the determinant-line normalisation
    fixes their scale.
    Three generations arise conditionally from the spinorial multiplicity reading of the invariant
    $\sigma_c(n_3) = 3$, a supplied selection rule (O23 proves the three-dimensionality of the neutral sector of a
    supplied spinor carrier, Theorem~3.1; the carrier selection and the identification of $\Sigma_c$ with
    that dimension are open).
    The resulting factor $\Cgen$ is distinct from the Lorentz sector and from any spatial triplet: no source
    identifies the invariant with spatial directions.
    What is NOT supplied is the independent fermionic weak doublet: the tensor functors of the
    spinor bundle give Lorentz-typed adjoint and determinant data, and under \HSpin{} the Lorentz commutant of the
    irreducible Weyl factor is scalar, so no internal non-abelian factor acts on the same spinor factor.
    The two deposited candidates for the missing multiplicity carrier $M_L \simeq \mathbb{C}^2$
    --- the internal Hecke operator doublets and the erased history fibres --- are excluded by
    exact obstructions in the companion no-go note (NSR)~\cite{Beau2026nsr}, whose central result is
    the no-sequential-re-entry theorem: information erased by the projective channel cannot be
    recovered by any tower of subsequent projections.
    NSR's finite-stratum obstructions are unconditional (not conditional on \HSpin{}); its Lorentz-lock input is
    imported from Q14, where the spinor reading of the carrier is under \HSpin{}.
    The matter-sector weak doublet, the role that this note reads the factor $\Ew$ of \HWeak{} as playing (a reading
    of this note, stated by neither Q14 nor the no-go note), is therefore an explicitly open frontier,
    realisable through an architectural extension, such as a parallel substrate channel with an oriented
    central/Weyl datum or an independent new multiplicity carrier, which the no-go note does not exclude, and
    not a consequence of the deposited corpus.

    \medskip
    This sub-programme is the first paper of the Q-series that depends simultaneously on all
    four layers of the programme: the admissible Weil fibre structure on the supplied carrier
    (Notes~1 and~5), the conditional Lorentzian geometry (Note~2), the gauge bundle for a supplied
    compact structure group (Note~3), and the conditional gauge--gravity
    system (Q13~\cite{Beau2026q13}, summarised in Presentation Note~8).

  \section{Logical Chain of the Sub-Programme}
    \label{sec:chain}

    The sub-programme is organised around the following structural chain, whose first arrow is read
    on the supplied Heisenberg carrier of Section~\ref{sec:motivation} rather than derived from the
    admissibility axioms, and whose second arrow is a modelling step.

    \begin{equation}
      \label{eq:chain}
      \Pi \Rightarrow F_n \simeq \Vr
      \;\overset{\text{model}}{\leadsto}\;
      \Mp(2,\mathbb{R})
      \;\Rightarrow\;
      \mathrm{mp}(2,\mathbb{R})_\mathbb{C} \simeq \mathfrak{sl}_2(\mathbb{C})
      \;\overset{\HSpin}{\leadsto}\;
      \Spin(3,1) \simeq \SL(2,\mathbb{C})
      \;\Rightarrow\;
      \Sbundle
    \end{equation}
    The arrow marked \HSpin{} is not an implication: it is Hypothesis~2.2 of Q14, supplied by no source.
    Given \HSpin{} and the distinct weak factor of \HWeak{} (Hypothesis~2.3 of Q14), the readings of the tensor
    sectors, of chirality and of generation multiplicity are:
    \[
      \underbrace{\mathrm{Sym}^2(S_L) \simeq \mathrm{ad}(P_\Pi),\ \wedge^2(S_L)\ \text{trivial}}
        _{\HSpin\text{: Lorentz sector}}
      \;,\quad
      \underbrace{\mathrm{Sym}^2(\Ew) \Rightarrow \adC(\su(2)_L)}_{\HWeak\text{: }\SU(2)_L}
      \;,\quad
      \underbrace{\wedge^2(\Ew) = L_Y}_{\HWeak\text{: }\UU(1)_Y}
      \;,
    \]
    \[
      \underbrace{\Epi \text{ left-admissible}}_{\text{Lorentz chirality, }\HSpin}
      \;,\quad
      \underbrace{\text{left-handed doublet assignment}}_{V-A,\ \HWeak}
      \;,\quad
      \underbrace{\sigma_c(n_3)=3 \to \Cgen}_{\text{3 generations (supplied rule)}}.
    \]

    Three distinct mechanisms contribute.

    \begin{enumerate}
      \item \textit{The complexified metaplectic algebra is $\mathfrak{sl}_2(\mathbb{C})$; its spin reading is
      \HSpin{}.}
      The Weil module $\Vr$ of the supplied carrier has a canonical symplectic structure, and its centre-preserving
      automorphisms act through the finite group $\SL(2,\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z})$, which has no Lie algebra.
      A real metaplectic model $\Mp(2,\mathbb{R})$ and the action of its Lie algebra on a two-dimensional doublet
      are therefore supplied model data.
      Given them, the complexification $\mathrm{mp}(2,\mathbb{R})_\mathbb{C} \simeq \mathfrak{sl}_2(\mathbb{C})$ is a
      statement about Lie algebras: it is algebraic and independent of any metric, and its simply connected
      integration is $\SL(2,\mathbb{C}) \simeq \Spin(3,1)$.
      An abstract isomorphism does not make this group the spin group of the effective spacetime.
      That requires a Lorentzian co-metric on the effective base and an identification of the $\SL(2,\mathbb{C})$
      acting on the doublet with the group covering the Lorentz frames of that co-metric, which is \HSpin{}.
      The spinor bundle $\Sbundle = S_L \oplus S_R$ is the associated bundle under \HSpin{}.

      \item \textit{The tensor functors are algebra on the supplied doublet; their readings are two distinct
      hypotheses.}
      $\mathrm{Sym}^2(\mathbb{C}^2) \simeq \mathfrak{sl}_2(\mathbb{C})$ is the complex adjoint and
      $\wedge^2(\mathbb{C}^2) \simeq \det(\mathbb{C}^2)$ the determinant line, trivial on $\SL(2,\mathbb{C})$.
      On the spinor factor under \HSpin{}, $\mathrm{Sym}^2(S_L)$ is the Lorentz sector and $\wedge^2(S_L)$ a trivial
      line that carries no hypercharge: neither is electroweak data.
      The electroweak reading applies the same functors to a distinct rank-two factor $\Ew$ with structure group
      $\UU(2)$ (\HWeak{}): $\mathrm{Sym}^2(\Ew)$ contains the complex weak adjoint, as an $\SU(2)$-module (as a
      $\UU(2)$-bundle it is that adjoint twisted by $L_Y$), from which the compact real form selects $\su(2)_L$, and
      $L_Y := \wedge^2(\Ew)$ supports $\UU(1)_Y$, non-trivially only because the structure
      group is $\UU(2)$.
      By the calibration of~\cite{Beau2026nsr}, no such factor is produced by the functors on $\Sbundle$: a fermionic
      weak doublet requires a multiplicity factor $M_L \simeq \mathbb{C}^2$ commuting with the Lorentz action,
      the Lorentz commutant of the irreducible Weyl factor being scalar under \HSpin{}.
      In the reading of this note $\Ew$ is the role such a factor would play: it is posited by \HWeak{} and not
      constructed.

      \item \textit{The projective endomorphism $\Epi$ gives Lorentz chirality; the weak chiral selection and the
      hypercharge constraints need \HWeak{} as well.}
      The projected Dirac operator, under \HSpin{}, satisfies the Lichnerowicz-type identity
      \[
        D^2_{\Pi,g,A}
        = -(\nabla^{S,A})^2 + \frac{R}{4} + \frac{1}{2}\gamma^\mu\gamma^\nu F^a_{\mu\nu} T^a_R
        + \Epi.
      \]
      Under \HSpin{} and the Born--Infeld datum (H1)--(H2) of O30, Q14~\cite{Beau2026q14} derives a unique
      antilinear lift of the parity that reverses chirality.
      Left-admissibility ($P_R \Epi P_R = 0$) is the definition of the orientation-compatible branch of Q14
      (Proposition~3.8), an input that the lift does not select.
      This is a statement about the projective residue and the Lorentz chirality $\gamma_5$.
      The $V-A$ structure is a different statement: the weak gauge action is carried by the left-handed factor
      alone, which is the representation content of \HWeak{}, and Q14 does not derive it from left-admissibility
      (Remark~3.9 of Q14).
      Under \HSpin{}, \HWeak{} and symbol compatibility, $\UU(1)_Y$ invariance of $\SPI[g,A,D_{\Pi,g,A}]$ imposes the
      anomaly-cancellation trace constraint $\mathrm{Tr}_{\Sbundle}(\gamma_5 Y A_\Pi(x)) = 0$ on the hypercharge
      weights, in its gauge-curvature part; the $\Epi$-dependent traces are not analysed in Q14.
      That constraint carries no colour hypothesis.
      The supplied three-dimensional colour module enters at the next level, that of the rigidity of
      the weights: the anomaly sums are weighted by the representation dimension, so the colour
      multiplicity enters them.
      Selecting the Standard Model pattern from those constraints is a further step, under the
      additional premise that $L_Y = \wedge^2(\Ew)$ carries the minimal integral normalisation compatible with the
      $\UU(1)_Y$ action, on the Standard Model content without a right-handed neutrino and with $Y_e \neq 0$, and then
      only up to an overall sign, rescaling and the exchange of the labels $u_R$ and $d_R$; the cubic constraint
      vanishes on that pattern precisely at colour multiplicity three.
    \end{enumerate}

    \subsection{Q14: Toward fermionic matter from projective Dirac admissibility}
      \label{sec:q14}

      \textbf{Q14}~\cite{Beau2026q14} is the sole constituent paper.
      It is organised around three independent but mutually compatible results, whose hypotheses differ.

      \textit{Theorem~A: Spinorial electroweak bundle} (algebra proved on supplied model data; two conditional
      readings).
      (a)~On the supplied Heisenberg carrier, given a real metaplectic model and a supplied doublet $V$, the
      complexified metaplectic algebra is $\mathfrak{sl}_2(\mathbb{C})$, with
      \[
        \mathrm{Sym}^2(V) \simeq \mathfrak{sl}_2(\mathbb{C}), \qquad \wedge^2(V) \simeq \mathbb{C}_{\det},
      \]
      the adjoint and the trivial line; this is algebraic, proved without either hypothesis, and independent of any
      metric.
      (b)~Under \HSpin{}, $\Sbundle = S_L \oplus S_R$ is a Dirac spinor bundle of the effective Lorentzian co-metric,
      $\mathrm{Sym}^2(S_L) \simeq \adC(P_\Pi)$ is its Lorentz sector, and $\wedge^2(S_L)$ is trivial.
      (c)~Under \HWeak{}, the same identities applied to $\Ew$ give the complex weak adjoint, with compact real form
      $\su(2)_L$, and $L_Y = \wedge^2(\Ew)$ carrying $\UU(1)_Y$:
      \[
        \mathrm{Sym}^2(\Ew) \Rightarrow \adC(\su(2)_L), \qquad \wedge^2(\Ew) = L_Y.
      \]
      This is Q14's own application of the tensor sectors to the weak factor; it does not import a
      gauge-group identification from the gauge structure sub-programme, which supplies none.
      Calibration: on the spinor factor these are Lorentz-typed adjoint and determinant data, and $\Ew$ is not
      constructed.
      In the reading of this note, $\Ew$ is the role of the independent fermionic weak doublet
      $M_L \simeq \mathbb{C}^2$, whose two deposited candidates are excluded by the exact obstructions
      of~\cite{Beau2026nsr}.
      NSR's finite-stratum obstructions are unconditional (not conditional on \HSpin{}); its Lorentz-lock input is
      imported from Q14, where the spinor reading of the carrier is under \HSpin{}.

      \textit{Theorem~B: Chiral selection and hypercharge constraints} (conditional; the parts carry different
      hypotheses).
      Under \HSpin{}, the projected Dirac operator $\Dpi$ has the Lichnerowicz-type square above.
      Under \HSpin{} and the Born--Infeld datum (H1)--(H2) of O30, the spinorial BI lift $\JBI$, whose internal
      part is $HK$, is, up to a phase, the

        Until such a sector exists, the quark bundle stays conditional.

        \item \textit{The fermionic weak multiplicity $M_L$ and the parallel-channel frontier.}
        The independent weak doublet carrier $M_L \simeq \mathbb{C}^2$ commuting with the
        Lorentz action, the role that this note reads the weak factor $\Ew$ of \HWeak{} as playing, is not derived by
        the corpus, and its two deposited candidates are excluded exactly~\cite{Beau2026nsr}: the internal Hecke
        operator doublets have no fermionic lift (the Weil carrier commutant is spanned by the parity projectors), and
        the erased history fibres are structurally abelian, their alternating line reaching the
        protected odd operator sector only through a re-entry that the no-sequential-re-entry
        theorem ($\pi v=0 \Rightarrow Q\pi v=0$ for the canonical record quotient) forbids to
        every projection tower.
        The surviving formulation for that route is architectural: a parallel substrate channel,
        refining the observable record and carrying an orientation of the central and Weyl
        generators, would realise the history-derived odd operator and reopen the activation of
        the protected sector.
        These are two new ingredients --- two new axioms, or one new axiom supplying both; the
        frontier is explicitly an extension question, not a derivation question, and an
        independent new multiplicity carrier outside the deposited corpus remains a distinct,
        unconstrained extension architecture.

        \item \textit{Neutrino identity as a projective test case.}
        Under \HWeak{}, the weak flavour basis and the mass propagation basis are not aligned, and in the present
        framework
        there is no structural reason for the weak flavour basis to diagonalize the projected spectral mass
        residue: basis misalignment is the generic situation, and alignment requires an additional projective
        stabiliser.
        This reverses the usual reading of mixing: the large PMNS mixing is the unfixed default, in line with
        the anarchy hypothesis~\cite{HallMurayamaWeiner2000}, while the near-diagonality of the CKM matrix
        becomes the non-trivial feature to be explained, requiring the charged projection channels to pin the
        up-type and down-type mass bases to a common projective frame.
        The full development --- the neutral-meson comparison, the ontological status of elementary
        misalignment~\cite{Hobart2026OnticTypes}, the Dirac/Majorana reading, and the structural test relating
        mixing magnitude to the number of identity-stabilising projection channels --- is carried by the
        companion note~\cite{Beau2026npi}.

        \item \textit{The Lorentzian spin solder.}
        Constructing the principal $\Spin(3,1)$-bundle of \HSpin{} from the metaplectic action, with a four-dimensional
        Lorentzian co-metric on the effective base, is open: the geometric branch supplies only a rank-three degenerate
        co-metric, under its hypotheses, and no source relates the metaplectic action to its frames.
        The same hypothesis would be needed to read the central Weil lift $R_b = W(-I)$ as the central element of the
        spin group, which requires a group-level map from the finite Weil group to the $\SL(2,\mathbb{C})$ of
        \HSpin{}; no such map is supplied, and whether the chirality grading removes the residual obstruction without a
        spacetime parity is open.
        Equally open is whether the admissible fibre supplies either of the two factors of \HSpin{} and \HWeak{}.

        \item \textit{The generation carrier and the level-to-generation map.}
        The operators of Q14 \S6 are model operators on $\Cgen = \mathrm{Sym}^2(V_{\mathrm{gen}})$, with
        $V_{\mathrm{gen}}$ a supplied doublet distinct from the soldered one.
        Reading the model operator $\mathrm{diag}(1,\tfrac12+u,\tfrac12-u)$ as the restriction of $\Epi^2$ is
        \HRes{}, which is not supplied, and the level-to-generation map is not established; the split value
        $\varepsilon=1/10$ is a matching value.
      \end{enumerate}

  \section{Open Deliverables}
    \label{sec:open}

    Under \HSpin{} and the Born--Infeld datum (H1)--(H2) of O30, the spinorial BI lift is derived in
    Q14~\cite{Beau2026q14}: the spinorial BI lift $\JBI$ dresses the BI parity by the weight grading ($HK$ on its
    internal part).
    This does not make Theorems~B and~C unconditional.
    Theorem~B carries \HSpin{} for Lorentz chirality and \HWeak{} for the $V-A$ structure and the anomaly
    constraints; Theorem~C carries the supplied rank-three carrier, the hypercharge weight rigidity of Theorem~B
    carries the supplied colour module, and the selection of the Standard Model pattern carries in addition the
    minimal integral normalisation of $L_Y$, whose connection to that pattern Q14 keeps among its own open directions.
    Two open deliverables remain.

    \textbf{Open deliverable 1: Explicit $\Epi$ and the Yukawa sector.}
    The mass spectrum of charged leptons and quarks, and the CKM/PMNS mixing matrices,
    require the explicit spectral construction of $\Epi$ as an operator on the admissible
    Hilbert space.
    This requires integrating the projected Yukawa Lagrangian $\LYuk$ and
    extracting the mass matrix from the eigenvalues of $\Epi^2$.
    This is the primary open problem of the fermionic sector.
    The structural form of $\Epi$ is fixed as the Schur complement of the eliminated spinorial
    block~\cite{Beau2026prs};
    the open part is its explicit Lorentzian block $1-P(s)$ along the $J_\Pi$-odd modulus.
    The symmetric finite front is $J_\Pi$-equivariant and gives $u_{\mathrm{fin}}=0$, but the discrete
    chiral-frontier normalisation $\mathcal N_A$ is fixed maximally, $\mathcal N_A=\theta_{\max}$, in the
    inherited central-phase lift~\cite{Beau2026cho}.
    The residual reflection $R_b=\mathrm{FT}^2$ is the central Weil lift.
    Reading it as central in the spin group, hence commuting with $\gamma_5$, needs a group-level map from the finite
    Weil group to the $\SL(2,\mathbb{C})$ of \HSpin{} that is not supplied, and Q14 states no commutation without
    it~\cite{Beau2026q14}; Q11OF takes $R_b$ to be central in the spin group~\cite{Beau2026q11of}.
    The split amplitude is therefore matched at A4 between this discrete normalisation and the
    Lorentzian Born--Infeld curvature $\mu_\chi^2=\partial_s^2\mathcal B(0)$, neither ingredient reducing
    to the other:
    \[
      |u| = \mathrm{A4Match}\!\left(\mathcal N_A,\,\mu_\chi^2\right).
    \]
    The operator side of this matching rests on the Schur transversality of~\cite{Beau2026prs}, established in the
    \HSpin{} stratum on the two premises of that note, symbol compatibility and the identification of $Z=[X,Y]$
    with the timelike direction
    after the co-metric completion $g^{\mu\nu}=2\eta^{\mu\nu}$; its normal form on $\Cgen$ is read here under \HRes{}.
    Under those premises the projected commutator $[\partial_s P|_0,J_\Pi]\big|_{\mathrm{span}(e_+,e_-)}$ has the exact
    metaplectic opening $\alpha(t,s)=ts\,r/\sinh r\not\equiv0$ with timelike Clifford symbol off the zero-mode locus.
    In the Schur-transverse branch, with a symbol-compatible spin frame, the A4 chiral polarisation is electric,
    hence $\mathcal P_{\mu\nu}\mathcal P^{\mu\nu}<0$, $\mu_\chi^2<0$, and the split opens
    spontaneously~\cite{Beau2026bim}.
    What remains open is the split value $|u|$ and the Yukawa sector downstream of $u$.
    On the real cascade of the model the A4 companion resolves the boundary-versus-interior selection in favour of
    saturation: the transverse (spin-two) cubic response of $\mathcal P\cdot\mathcal R_\chi$ vanishes
    path-invariantly at $\gamma=0$, so the amplitude mechanism is Born--Infeld saturation, and the sixth-order
    interior lock is doubly conditional --- on a non-derived complex phase and a non-prescribed ordering --- hence not
    concluded~\cite{Beau2026bim}.
    The value $|u|$ itself is not a parameter-free prediction: it is dictionary-bound through the chiral-frontier
    normalisation $\mathcal N_A$, the angular analogue of the radial scale $\kappa=5/12$ of the exit-deficit
    dictionary~\cite{Beau2026aar, Beau2026q14}, whose split value $\varepsilon=1/10$ is fixed by matching the
    three-level exit-deficit structure of the model operators to the external ADE ratio of a selected
    case~\cite{Beau2026a4}.
    The genuine first-principles front for the value is therefore upstream and structural, not a numerical amplitude:
    the ADE case selection and the level-to-generation map, the derivation of the projective-resolution growth and the
    cascade exponent $\beta$, and the derivation of the transfer
    constant $N_{\mathrm{casc}}$ that fixes $\mathcal N_A$~\cite{Beau2026q14}.
    No structural bound on $\beta$ is available: the Born--Infeld flux constraint together with the
    Cheeger inequality for Ramanujan--LPS graphs does not yield one, and repairing that route needs
    structural choices absent from the Born--Infeld axiom itself~\cite{Beau2026a8}.
    Deriving the projective-resolution growth $\Lambda_{\mathrm{proj}}(n)$ from first principles is
    the central open problem of that front~\cite{Beau2026a4}.
    What remains quantitative is therefore these upstream dictionary inputs, the integral normalisation of
    $\LYuk$, and generation mixing, for which Q14 excludes a complex metaplectic phase as a source of

    $\mathfrak{sl}_2(\mathbb{C})$~\cite{Beau2026q14}; the internal block is treated in~\cite{Beau2026pyo}, where
    the $J_\Pi$-odd anti-Hermitian part $A_\Pi$ of the lift vanishes, a statement about $A_\Pi$ as defined
    (a generator, not the anomaly density of Q14).

    \paragraph{Charged-lepton unit-dispersion target.}
    The phenomenological target for the open charged-lepton masses is stated most sharply not as a mass ratio but
    as a scale-free shape invariant: the Koide relation is equivalent to unit relative dispersion of the
    square-root mass vector $r_\ell = (\sqrt{m_e}, \sqrt{m_\mu}, \sqrt{m_\tau})$, since
    $Q_\ell = (1 + \mathrm{CV}(r_\ell)^2)/3$, so that $Q_\ell = 2/3 \iff \mathrm{CV}(r_\ell) = 1$
    (standard deviation equal to mean).
    The generation ladder $(1, \tfrac12 + u, \tfrac12 - u)$ of the model operator has bounded dispersion,
    $\mathrm{CV} \le 1/\sqrt2$ and $Q \le 1/2$, and therefore cannot by itself reach the Koide value; the dispersion
    constraint and the concrete hierarchy (the placement of one generation near a node of the square-root profile)
    separate.
    KUD records this target invariant, the open maximum-entropy test, and the scoped linear self-adjoint
    determinant no-go described above~\cite{Beau2026kud}.

    The Yukawa sector splits into two sub-fronts.
    The coupling line is conditional on \HWeak{}: the determinant line $L_Y = \wedge^2(\Ew)$ is the one-dimensional
    functor among the symmetric and exterior powers of the weak factor, invisible to the $\SU(2)_L$ adjoint sector and
    carrying the abelian weight because the structure group of $\Ew$ is $\UU(2)$~\cite{Beau2026pyl}.
    The generation levels are read on the model operator
    $\mathrm{diag}(1, \tfrac12 + u, \tfrac12 - u)$ on $\Cgen$, which is $\Epi^2|_{\Cgen}$ only under \HRes{}:

    the three generations needs the level-to-generation map, which is not established.
    The Yukawa coupling $\Ypi$ is, schematically, a coupling between the left-handed sector
    $P_L \Sbundle \otimes \Ew \otimes L_Y^{k}$ and the right-handed sector $P_R \Sbundle \otimes L_Y^{m}$ of \HWeak{}
    (with \HSpin{} for $P_L$, $P_R$);
    as a sesquilinear Lorentz-invariant form it needs the weak linking carrier $K$ of \HFac{}, and how the weak
    doublet index is absorbed is not constructed here.
    On the generation block $\Ygen$, under \HRes{} and \HSq{}, the squared observable $H_\Pi := \Ygen^\dagger\Ygen$ has
    $\mathrm{Spec}(H_\Pi)|_{\Cgen} = \lambda_Y^2\,\{1, \tfrac12 + u, \tfrac12 - u\}$, so $\Epi^2|_{\Cgen}$ determines
    the squared Yukawa levels, not the morphism~\cite{Beau2026pyo}.
    What stays open is the chiral polar factor $U_\Pi$ in $\Ygen = U_\Pi H_\Pi^{1/2}$ --- a unitary chiral map on the
    support of $H_\Pi$ --- which carries the chiral orientation and the complex mixing phase, together with the separate

    $\Epi^2$ is blind to $U_\Pi$: under \HRes{} and \HSq{} it fixes $H_\Pi = \Ygen^\dagger\Ygen$ but fixes $\Ygen$

    rephasings; its

    These statements concern the generation block; they transfer to the full coupling only under \HFac{} of

  \section{Conclusion}
    \label{sec:conclusion}

    The fermionic matter sub-programme states what the fermionic sector requires when fermions are sought in the
    admissible Weil fibre rather than added to the Cosmochrony framework as external fields.
    On a supplied Heisenberg carrier, given a real metaplectic model and a supplied doublet, the algebra is proved
    and uses no metric: the complexified metaplectic algebra is $\mathfrak{sl}_2(\mathbb{C})$, the symmetric square of
    the doublet is the adjoint and its exterior square the determinant line.
    The carrier is supplied rather than selected by the admissibility axioms, and everything below
    inherits that condition.
    Two physical readings of the algebra are distinct hypotheses, each a missing identification and neither excluded
    by any result: \HSpin{}, which identifies the $\SL(2,\mathbb{C})$ of the doublet with the spin group of a
    four-dimensional Lorentzian co-metric on the effective base, and \HWeak{}, which supplies a distinct rank-two weak
    factor $\Ew$ with structure group $\UU(2)$.

    Under \HSpin{}, $\Sbundle = S_L \oplus S_R$ is a spinor bundle of the effective co-metric, $\mathrm{Sym}^2(S_L)$ is
    the Lorentz sector and $\wedge^2(S_L)$ a trivial line (Theorem~A).
    Under \HWeak{}, the electroweak data of $\SU(2)_L \times \UU(1)_Y$ arise from the same tensor functors applied to
    $\Ew$: $\su(2)_L \subset \mathrm{Sym}^2(\Ew)$ and $L_Y = \wedge^2(\Ew)$.
    The independent fermionic weak doublet, the role that this note reads $\Ew$ as playing, is not produced by the
      functors on
    $\Sbundle$ and not constructed, its two deposited candidates being excluded at the operator and history strata
    by the companion no-go note~\cite{Beau2026nsr}.
    Lorentz chirality, the left-admissibility of the projective endomorphism $\Epi$, is the definition of the
    orientation-compatible branch of Q14, an input, under \HSpin{} and the Born--Infeld datum of O30 (Theorem~B).
    The $V-A$ structure is a different statement, the representation content of \HWeak{}, and it is not derived from
    left-admissibility.
    Under both hypotheses, the anomaly-cancellation trace constrains the hypercharge weights with no colour
    hypothesis; their rigidity is what carries a supplied three-dimensional colour module.
    Selecting the Standard Model pattern among them takes a further premise, the minimal integral
    normalisation of the determinant line $L_Y = \wedge^2(\Ew)$, holds only up to an overall sign, rescaling and the
    exchange of $u_R$ and $d_R$, and is where the cubic constraint vanishes at colour multiplicity three; Q14 leaves
    the explicit connection between that normalisation and the pattern open.
    The constraints are the gauge-curvature ones, the $\Epi$-dependent traces not being analysed.
    Three generations arise from the spinorial multiplicity reading of $\sigma_c(n_3) = 3$
    (Theorem~C, conditional on the supplied rank-three selection rule of O23).
    The colour-coupled quark sector follows by tensoring with $V_{\mathrm{color}}$, and carries that
    same supplied module: no admissible colour sector is derived~\cite{Beau2026a35}.
    The matter-sector weak doublet stands as the explicitly architectural frontier of the
    sub-programme: its realisation through the deposited operator or history structures
    requires a parallel substrate channel with an oriented central/Weyl datum --- two new
    ingredients, or one new axiom supplying both, that no deposited principle provides ---
    while an independent new multiplicity carrier remains a distinct extension architecture
    (Section~\ref{sec:open-problems}).

    On the model operators of Q14 \S6, the inter-generation splitting is localised: statically obstructed, living in a
    two-dimensional $J_\Pi$-odd lift sector, and vanishing on the symmetric finite front ($u_{\mathrm{fin}}=0$,
    $J_\Pi$-equivariant).
    Reading the residual reflection $R_b=\mathrm{FT}^2$ as central in the spin group, hence commuting with $\gamma_5$,
    needs a group-level map from the finite Weil group to the $\SL(2,\mathbb{C})$ of \HSpin{}, which is not
    supplied.
    The amplitude mechanism of the splitting is Born--Infeld saturation on the real cascade of the
    model~\cite{Beau2026bim}.
    The generation sector is described by model operators on a distinct supplied doublet, the identification \HRes{}
    of which with restrictions of $\Epi^2$ is not supplied, and the level-to-generation map is not established.
    The remaining open components are the explicit Lorentzian $\Epi$, whose eliminated-block jet $1-P(s)$ is
    structurally constrained and whose $J_\Pi$-odd tangent has left-handed block equal to half the transported
    equivariance defect rate, $[\Pi_{J_\Pi\text{-odd}}\dot Q(0)]_{LL}=\tfrac12\partial_s\Delta_\chi(P)|_0$, in the
    companion
    note~\cite{Beau2026ebj} (one block of the split-sourcing tangent, not a unique generator); the split value, which is
    dictionary-bound and whose first-principles derivation is upstream and structural (the ADE case selection and
    level-to-generation map, the cascade exponent $\beta$, and the transfer constant $N_{\mathrm{casc}}$); and the
    derivation of the Yukawa sector and mass spectrum.

    The local operator chain is constrained but not closed in the eliminated-block and saturation
    companions~\cite{Beau2026ebj, Beau2026bim}: for a covariant family the split rate is sourced at first order by the
    odd tangent $[A_-,Q_0]$ together with the first-order motion of the spinorial embedding, and under the localisation
    hypothesis that this source is exhausted by the transported left-handed block $\tfrac12\partial_s\Delta_\chi(P)|_0$,
    the embedding-motion terms and the chirally off-diagonal part of the tangent contributing nothing to the $J_3$
    component, the saturation-margin note factorises the contact split conditionally as
    \[
      u_\partial = \sqrt{2}\,\sigma\,s_*\,\frac{T_{J_3}(\partial_s\Delta_\chi(P)|_0)}{\langle J_3, J_3\rangle},
    \]
    where the even-block factor $\sqrt{2}\,\sigma$ comes from the algebraic value
    $\mathrm{diag}(1,\tfrac12,\tfrac12)$ of the even part of the model operator, read as $E_0^2|_{\Cgen}$ under \HRes{},
    $\sigma=\pm1$ being the sign of $u$,
    $s_*$ is the radial A4 contact, and $T_{J_3}$ is the oriented angular observable whose normalised evaluation is
    dictionary-bound through $\mathcal{N}_A$.
    The localisation hypothesis is open and is not satisfied by the explicit family of the companion, whose
    auxiliary-compression rate differs from its split rate; with the selection of the covariant family and the
    normalisation of the defect rate also open, the remaining magnitude problem is upstream in its dictionary
    inputs and local in the localisation of the odd tangent.

    The mass-sector frontier separates into three layers.
    On model operators, the angular Weil--BFS coefficient is closed in the standard campaign
    ($\theta_{\max}=2\pi/(3q)$)~\cite{Beau2026aar}, and, on the premises of~\cite{Beau2026prs}, in the \HSpin{}
    stratum, and under \HRes{}, the
    Schur transport with the electric genus of~\cite{Beau2026bim} gives $u\neq0$.
    The external block $R_{\mathrm{mix}}$ is not reached by the image of $\mathfrak{sl}_2(\mathbb{C})$ (Q14,
    Remark~6.4), and $A_\Pi$, the anti-Hermitian part of the $J_\Pi$-odd part of its lift, vanishes as defined
    in~\cite{Beau2026pyo} (a generator, not the anomaly density of Q14), which claims no physical exclusion of the
    internal block.
    Finally, the dictionary value $\varepsilon=1/10$ is not a capacity fit and is not fixed by
    $\mathrm{Sym}^2(\mathbb{C}^2)$ alone; after the capacity and $\mathrm{Sym}^2$ normalisations are separated, the
    remaining datum is the ADE / type-rigidity ratio $R=3/2$ --- equivalently the prime $5$, or the
    $\sqrt{5}$-gate --- whose selection over the neutral order-four convention ($R=\tfrac53$, $\varepsilon=\tfrac18$)
    is the single residual lock~\cite{Beau2026aog}.

    The neutrino sector provides a particularly sharp interpretive test case: if particle identity is projective
    rather than primitive, then PMNS-like misalignment is the generic neutral case, while CKM near-alignment is
    the structure requiring explanation (Section~\ref{sec:open-problems}).

    \paragraph{Interpretive outlook.}
    The following is a reading of the results, not a result.
    The Standard Model does not treat spin and weak isospin as one structure: a left-handed electron is a Lorentz
    spinor and, separately, a member of a weak doublet.
    The sub-programme reaches the same separation from the other side.
    A supplied doublet on the admissible fibre carries one copy of the algebra, and that algebra is exactly the algebra
    of both spin and weak isospin, but one copy cannot carry both roles.
    Where the fermionic sector meets spacetime and where it meets the weak interaction are therefore two distinct
    questions, and the question of why weak interactions are left-handed becomes the question of how the two factors
    are joined.
    The remaining problems are specific: the two hypotheses on how the fibre is soldered, and the rigidity problems
    for $\Epi$ and $L_Y$.
